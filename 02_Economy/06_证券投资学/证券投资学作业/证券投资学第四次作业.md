1. 2024年中国国内生产总值（GDP）同比增长5.0%，达到1349084亿元人民币，显示经济稳步增长。然而，2025年2月消费者价格指数（CPI）同比下降0.7%，表明通缩压力加大。根据美林投资时钟模型，所以可以大致推断目前中国经济处于“衰退期”，即经济增长放缓且通胀下行的阶段。在此阶段，应该配置债券。
2. 近期，人民币汇率的波动对中国资本市场产生了多方面影响。2025年初，人民币对美元汇率贬值1.3%，接近16个月低点，美元兑人民币约为7.33元。 这一贬值趋势可能削弱国际投资者对中国资产的信心，导致资本外流，增加资本市场的波动性。但是同时，人民币贬值也可能提升中国出口产品的价格竞争力，促进出口导向型企业的盈利能力，进而对相关上市公司股价产生积极影响。综合上面两点,人民币汇率的变化对中国资本市场的影响具有复杂性，需结合具体行业和市场环境进行深入分析。
3. 在衰退阶段，政府通常通过扩张性财政政策（如增加支出、减税）与宽松货币政策（如降息、量化宽松）刺激总需求，促进经济复苏，此时债券收益率下降、股票市场有复苏预期而回暖，大宗商品价格可能仍低迷。在复苏阶段，政策仍偏宽松，支持就业和增长，企业盈利改善带动股市上涨，大宗商品需求随之回升。在过热阶段，为了抑制通胀，政府可能收紧财政支出，央行提高利率，货币政策转向紧缩，股市和债市承压，大宗商品价格可能因通胀预期而上升。在滞胀阶段，经济增长放缓但物价仍高，政策应对较为棘手，可能采取结构性财政调整和有选择的货币干预，此时证券市场波动加剧，大宗商品因抗通胀特性可能受益，而债券和股票双双承压.
4. 在当前阶段，中国经济面临“低增长+通缩压力”并存的局面，通胀波动虽不剧烈，但长期通胀预期与全球价格变量仍可能带来冲击。因此，资产配置上应注重对冲通胀不确定性与提升实际回报率。第一，配置抗通胀属性较强的实物资产（如黄金、房地产相关股票）可提供价值锚定；第二，可适当增加大宗商品ETF或资源类企业股票的权重，以捕捉输入型通胀带来的收益机会；第三，债券配置应倾向短久期或通胀挂钩债券（如CPI联动债），以减少利率上行带来的价格风险；同时，适度配置海外资产（如美元资产），可对冲人民币贬值及全球价格输入的影响，形成多元化的风险屏障。
5. 先行指标是指在经济总体趋势发生变化前便已开始变动的指标，用于预测未来经济走势，典型如制造业PMI、新增社融、股票市场指数、消费者信心指数等。同步指标与经济活动几乎同时变动，反映当前经济状况，代表指标包括GDP增速、工业增加值、用电量、就业率等。而滞后指标则在经济趋势明确后才会发生变动，主要用于确认周期变化，其代表包括CPI、PPI、失业率、利率水平等。

1. In 2024, China's gross domestic product (GDP) grew by 5.0% year on year to 134.9084 trillion yuan, indicating steady economic growth. In February 2025, however, the consumer price index (CPI) fell by 0.7% year on year, pointing to stronger deflationary pressure. Under the Merrill Lynch investment-clock framework, this combination roughly places the economy in the recession phase, in which growth and inflation are both declining. A portfolio in this phase should therefore tilt toward bonds.
2. Recent renminbi exchange-rate movements have affected China's capital markets through several channels. At the start of 2025, the renminbi depreciated by 1.3% against the US dollar and approached a sixteen-month low, with one dollar worth about 7.33 yuan. Depreciation may weaken foreign investors' confidence in Chinese assets, encourage capital outflows, and increase market volatility. At the same time, it can improve the price competitiveness and profitability of export-oriented firms, supporting the share prices of relevant listed companies. The net effect is therefore industry- and market-specific.
3. During a recession, governments commonly use expansionary fiscal policy, such as higher spending or tax cuts, and accommodative monetary policy, such as rate cuts or quantitative easing, to support aggregate demand and recovery. Bond yields decline, equities may recover in anticipation of better conditions, and commodity prices may remain weak. During recovery, policy generally remains supportive, rising corporate earnings lift equities, and commodity demand strengthens. During overheating, fiscal and monetary policy may tighten to curb inflation, putting pressure on bonds and equities while inflation expectations support commodities. Stagflation combines weak growth with high prices, making policy especially difficult; targeted monetary intervention and structural fiscal adjustment may be used, market volatility rises, commodities may benefit as inflation hedges, and both bonds and equities may struggle.
4. The current combination of low growth and deflationary pressure is mild in its immediate inflation volatility, but longer-run inflation expectations and global price shocks still matter. Asset allocation should therefore hedge inflation uncertainty while protecting real returns. Inflation-hedging real assets, such as gold and property-related equities, can provide a store-of-value component. Commodity ETFs and resource-company shares can capture gains from imported inflation. Bond exposure can favor short-duration or inflation-linked securities, such as CPI-linked bonds, to limit losses if rates rise. A measured allocation to foreign, especially US-dollar, assets can also hedge renminbi depreciation and imported global price shocks.
5. Leading indicators begin to move before the overall economy turns and help forecast future conditions; examples include the manufacturing PMI, new aggregate social financing, stock-market indices, and consumer-confidence indices. Coincident indicators move broadly with current activity and include GDP growth, industrial value added, electricity consumption, and the employment rate. Lagging indicators change after a trend has become established and mainly confirm the cycle; examples include the CPI, PPI, unemployment rate, and interest-rate levels.
