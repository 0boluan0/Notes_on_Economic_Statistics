## 金融市场(Financial Market)（financial market）
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Financial Market*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

在金融市场中，资金从那些拥有闲置货币的人手中转移到资金紧缺的人手中。债券市场和股票市场等金融市场将资金从没有生产用途的人向有生产用途的人转移， 从而提高了经济效率。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
In financial markets, funds move from people with surplus money to people who need financing. Bond and stock markets raise economic efficiency by directing funds from those without a productive use for them to those who can put them to productive use.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 债券市场与利率
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Bond market and interest rate*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

* 证券（security, 又称金融工具） 是对发行人未来收入与资产（asset, 金融索取权或隶属于所有权的财产权） 的索取权。
* 债券（bond）是债务证券，它承诺在一个特定的时间段中进行定期支付。由于债券市场可以帮助政府和企业筹集到所需要的资金. 并且是决定利率的场所.因此在经济活动中有着重要的特殊意义。
* 利率（interest rate）是借款的成本或为借入资金支付的价格 （通常表示为借入 100 美元所支付的年利息占 100 美元的百分比）
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
* A security, also called a financial instrument, is a claim on an issuer's future income or assets. An asset may represent a financial claim or a property right arising from ownership.
* A bond is a debt security that promises periodic payments over a specified period. Bond markets are important because they allow governments and firms to raise funds and help determine interest rates.
* An interest rate is the cost of borrowing—the price paid for the use of funds. It is normally expressed as annual interest per 100 dollars borrowed, divided by those 100 dollars.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 辨析：bonds 、note、 bills
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Distinguishing Bonds, Notes, and Bills*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
* bonds：长期债券，一般都是十年以上那种
* note：中期，不长不短那种，一般在五年左右
* bills：短期，一年左右，
三者仅仅反映时间的不通过，其他的不同我们不考虑
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
* Bonds: long-term debt instruments, generally with maturities of ten years or more.
* Notes: medium-term debt instruments, here treated as having maturities of roughly five years.
* Bills: short-term debt instruments, here treated as having maturities of about one year.
For this course, the three labels distinguish maturity; other differences are set aside.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 图表题预训练
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Pre-training for chart and diagram questions*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

这类长期时间序列图通常可读出四点：
1. 三条利率序列长期同向波动（共振明显）。
2. Baa 级企业债利率通常最高（信用风险溢价最大）。
3. 长期政府债利率通常居中。
4. 3 个月国库券利率通常最低，且在货币政策变化阶段波动更快。
一般来讲想要得到所有的分数的话这一张图得讲大概4到5条
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Four main observations can normally be drawn from this type of long-run time-series chart:
1. The 3 interest-rate series display strong long-run co-movement.
2. Baa-rated corporate bonds normally have the highest yields because they carry the largest credit-risk premium.
3. Long-term government-bond yields normally lie in the middle.
4. The 3-month Treasury-bill rate is normally the lowest and responds more quickly during changes in monetary policy.
To receive full marks, an answer should normally make four or five substantive observations about the chart.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 普通股与优先股
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Common stock and preferred stock*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

* 普通股（common stock）, 通常简称为股票（stock）, 代表持有者对公司的所有权。股票是对公司收益和资产的索取权。a share of ownership in a corporation。
	* 另：在本课中认为equity=stock，二者不作区分。
* 优先股（preferred stock）一个相较于普通股的概念，没有投票权，但是可以优先于普通股得到应得的股息（dividend）抑或赔偿。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
* Common stock, usually shortened to *stock*, represents an ownership interest in a company and a claim on its earnings and assets—a share of ownership in a corporation.
    * In this course, *equity* and *stock* are treated as equivalent terms.
* Preferred stock, unlike common stock, normally carries no voting rights but has priority in receiving dividends and claims on assets in liquidation.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 辨析：shareholder、stockholder、stackholder
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Distinguishing Shareholder, Stockholder, and “Stackholder”*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

shareholder：持有股票的人，即股票持有者
stockholder：同理
stackholder：利益相关者
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Shareholder: a person who owns shares; in this context, the same as a stockholder.
Stockholder: the same concept as shareholder.
“Stackholder”: the source intends *stakeholder*, meaning a party with an interest in the company.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 金融中介
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Financial intermediation*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

* 金融中介（financial intermediaries, 从储蓄者手中吸收资金， 并向需要资金的企业或个人提供贷款） 间接进行这一活动。通俗来讲是二道贩子。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
* Financial intermediaries carry out this transfer indirectly: they obtain funds from savers and lend them to firms or individuals that need financing. In plain language, they are middlemen between lenders and borrowers.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 金融创新与金融危机
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Financial Innovation and Financial Crises*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

* 金融创新（financial innovation），创新好了有助于经济发展，但比较大可能会导致金融危机（eg。次贷危机）
* 金融危机（financial crisis）所谓金融危机是指金融市场出现混乱， 并伴随着资产价格的暴跌以及众多金融机构和非金融机构的破产。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
* Financial innovation can support economic development when it is well designed, but large or poorly controlled innovations can also contribute to a financial crisis, as in the subprime mortgage crisis.
* A financial crisis is a severe disruption in financial markets, accompanied by collapsing asset prices and the failure of many financial and non-financial institutions.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
