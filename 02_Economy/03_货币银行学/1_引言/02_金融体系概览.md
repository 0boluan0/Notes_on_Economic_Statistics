## 债务与股权市场(Debt and [[Equity Multiplier|Equity]] Markets)
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Debt and Equity Markets (Debt and [[Equity Multiplier|Equity]] Markets)*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 债务工具
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Debt Instruments*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

债务工具的**期限(maturity)** 就是距离该项工具最终偿还日的时间。
* 短期（short-term）债务工具：期限在一年以下
* 中期 ( intermediate-term) 债务工具：期限介于1至10年之内
* 长期 ( [[Long-term Debt Ratio|long]]-term) 债务工具：期限在十年或十年以上
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The **maturity** of debt instruments is the time remaining until the final repayment date.

- Short-term (short-term) debt instruments: Maturities less than one year.
- Intermediate-term (intermediate-term) debt instruments: Maturities between one and ten years.
- Long-term ([[Long-term Debt Ratio|long]]-term) debt instruments: Maturities of ten years or more.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
### 股权
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Equity*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

* 股权 ( equity) 工具承诺持有者按份额享有公司的净收益 (扣除费用和税款后的收入) 和资产。
	由于这种工具没有到期日. 因此被视为长期证券。拥有股票意味着你拥有该公司的一定份额. 因此你有权就公司的重大事项和董事长的选举进行投票。
* 股利（dividend）股权工具的持有者可以得到定期支付。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
* Equity tools commit holders to share in the net income (income after expenses and taxes) and assets of the company.
  Since this instrument has no maturity date, it is considered a long-term security. Owning stock means you own a portion of the company, giving you the right to vote on significant matters and the election of the chairman.
* Dividends are payments made periodically to holders of equity tools.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 一级市场和二级市场(Primary and Secondary Markets)
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*primary market and secondary market (Primary and Secondary Markets)*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 一级市场
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*primary market*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

不公开进行
一级市场 (primary market) 是筹集资金的公司或政府机构将其新发行的股票或债券等证券销售给最初购买者的金融市场。
* 投资银行 (investmentbank) 是一级市场上协助证券首次出售的重要的金融机构。
* 证券承销 ( underwriting)投资银行确保公司证券能够按照某一价格销售出去， 之后再向公
众推销这些证券。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Not publicly conducted
primary market (primary market) is the financial market where companies or government entities raise funds by selling new stocks or bonds to their initial purchasers.
* An investment bank (investmentbank) is an important financial institution that assists in the initial sale of securities on the primary market.
* Underwriting involves the investment bank ensuring that a company's securities can be sold at a certain price, after which they promote these securities to the public.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
### 二级市场
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*secondary market*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

二级市场 ( secondarymarket) 是交易已经发行的证券的金融市场。
* 经纪人 (broker) 是投资者的代理人， 它们负责匹配证券的买方和卖方.
* 交易商 (dealer) 按照报价买卖证券， 从而将买卖双方联结起来.
* 流动性(liquidity):投资者可以更加容易和快捷地出售金融工具， 实现筹措资金的目的.
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
secondary market (secondary market) is the financial market where already issued securities are traded.

* A broker acts as an agent for investors, matching buyers and sellers of securities.
* A dealer buys and sells securities at quoted prices to link buyers and sellers together.
* Liquidity: Investors can more easily and quickly sell financial instruments to raise funds.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 交易所与场外市场(Exchanges and [[Over-identified|Over]]-the-Counter Markets)
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Exchanges and Off-exchange Markets (Exchanges and [[Over-identified|Over]]-the-Counter Markets)*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 交易所（exchange）
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Exchange (exchange)*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

即证券的买卖双方 （或者他们的代理人或经纪人） 在一个集中的场所进行交易的二级市场。
	eg：纽约证券交易所和芝加哥商品交易所
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
That is, when buyers and sellers (or their agents or brokers) of securities conduct transactions in a centralized location, such as the New York Stock Exchange and the Chicago Mercantile Exchange.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
### 场外市场（[[Over-identified|over]]-the-countermarket, OTC）
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Over-The-Counter Market ([[Over-identified|over]] - OTC)*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

即分处各地的拥有证券存货的交易商随时向与其联系并愿意接受其报价的人在 “柜台” 上买卖证券的二级市场。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Traders holding securities located in different places can trade securities "over-the-counter" with those they are connected to and who are willing to accept their quotations, i.e., the secondary market.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

Trading in money market securities is more widespread and has greater liquidity.

### 美国国库券（United States Treasury Bills）
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*United States Treasury Bills*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

分为1，3，6个月，国库券到期支付的金额是固定的，不付息，但是是折价发行的，你可能用9000买10000面值的国库券。他不可能违约。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Treasury bills have maturities of 1, 3, or 6 months. They pay a fixed amount at maturity and no periodic interest, but are issued at a discount—for example, you might pay 9,000 for a bill with a face value of 10,000. They are treated as free of default risk.

*Negotiable Bank Certificates of Deposit (NegCDs)*

	是在二级市场上出售的存单
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
* Certificates of deposit ([[Condition Index|CI]]) are debt instruments issued by banks to depositors. The bank pays a fixed amount of interest each year and repays the depositor's original purchase price at maturity.

  Negotiable certificates of deposit are CDs that can be sold in the secondary market.

事实上是一种短期贷款（期限通常在两周以内）如果借款人不清偿贷款，国库券将作为抵押品归贷款人所有

*Federal Funds (Fed Funds)*

<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

即随着交易规模的扩大. 平摊在每一美元交易上的成本在降低。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
That is, as transaction sizes expand, the cost per dollar of a transaction is reduced.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

	eg：银行知道如何找到一名好律师来起草一份严密的贷款合同， 这份合同可以在其贷款活动中重复使用. 于是就降低了每笔交易的法律成本。银行不会花 500 美元购买一份贷款合同 (这可能还不够好)， 而是会花 5 000 美元去雇用一个一流的律师. 这个律师所设计的滴水不漏的合同可以供2 000 笔贷款使用 因此每笔贷款的成本就降低到了 2. 50 美元。这样由金融中介机构向卡尔发放 1 000 美元的贷款就变得有利可图。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For example, a bank knows how to hire a good lawyer to draft a rigorous loan contract that can be reused throughout its lending business, thereby reducing the legal cost of each transaction. Instead of paying $500 for a possibly inadequate contract, the bank can pay $5,000 for a first-rate lawyer. If that watertight contract is used for 2,000 loans, its cost falls to $2.50 per loan, making it profitable for a financial intermediary to lend Karl $1,000.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 流动性服务 (liquidity services)
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*liquidity services*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

是指由流动性服务商提供的一系列服务，旨在提高金融市场中资产的流动性。这些服务商可能是证券公司、专业机构或其他符合条件的组织，它们通过提供持续的双边报价（即同时报出证券的买入价和卖出价）来保证交易的及时成交，从而促进市场的流动性
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
It refers to a set of services provided by liquidity providers aimed at enhancing the liquidity of assets in financial markets. These service providers may include securities firms, professional institutions, or other eligible organizations that guarantee timely transactions through continuous bilateral quotes (simultaneously offering buy and sell prices for securities), thereby promoting market liquidity.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*risk sharing*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

金融中介机构所设计和提供的资产品种的风险在投资者所能承受的范围之内. 金融中介机构用销售这些资产所获取的资金去购买风险大得多的资产。

Financial intermediaries design and provide asset types whose risks fall within investors' tolerance levels. These intermediaries use the funds obtained from selling these assets to purchase assets with much greater risk.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 资产转换 (asset transformation)
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*asset transformation*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

低交易成本允许金融中介机构以较低的成本进行风险分担. 使得它们能够获取风险资产的收益与已出售资产的成本之间的差额. 这也是金融中介机构的利润。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->

### 多样化 ( diversification)
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->

 包括投资于收益变动方向不一致的一系列资产， 即资产组合 (portfolio), 从而将总体风险降低到单个资产的风险之下。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Diversification means investing in a collection of assets whose returns do not all move in the same direction—that is, a portfolio—so that total risk falls below the risk of holding a single asset.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 信息不对称 ( asymmetric  information)
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*information asymmetry (asymmetric information)*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

在金融市场上, 交易一方对另一方缺乏充分的了解. 以至于无法做出正确的决策。这种不对等的状态被称为信息不对称 。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
In financial markets, one party lacks sufficient understanding of another, making it impossible to make correct decisions. This imbalanced state is known as information asymmetry.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 逆向选择 (adverse selection)
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*adverse selection (adverse selection)*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

金融市场上的逆向选择是指. 那些最可能造成不利 (逆向) 后果即制造信贷风险的潜在借款人往往是那些最积极寻求贷款， 并且最可能获取贷款的人。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
In financial markets, adverse selection means that the prospective borrowers most likely to produce an adverse outcome—that is, to create credit risk—are often the ones who seek loans most actively and are most likely to obtain them.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 道德风险 ( moral hazard)
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*moral hazard (moral hazard)*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

金融市场中的道德风险是借款人可能从事不利于贷款人的 (不道德) 的活动， 因为这些活动加大了贷款无法清偿的概率. 因而造成了风险 (危险）
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
moral hazard in the financial market refers to activities that a borrower might engage in that are detrimental to the lender (unethical). These activities increase the likelihood of default on the loan, thereby creating risk.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

These financial institutions possess characteristics of mutual funds while also offering accounts that resemble deposits; hence, they function quite similarly to deposit institutions. The manner in which they obtain funds is similar to mutual funds, after which they purchase safer and more liquid instruments of the money market.

One of their most important features is that shareholders can issue checks based on the value of their holdings.

<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 投资银行（investment bank）
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*investment bank*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

不吸收资金也不发放贷款。为企业证券发行提供帮助。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
First, it recommends the types of securities that a company may issue (stocks or bonds). Subsequently, it purchases these securities from the issuing company at predetermined prices and sells them in the market, thereby helping the company to sell its securities (underwrite).
Investment banks also earn fees by helping firms acquire or merge with other firms.
