 
# 什么是金融危机(What Is a Financial Crisis)

*What Is a Financial Crisis?*

* 金融脆弱性financial frictions(Financial Frictions):信息不对称问题阻碍了资本的有效分配

* **Financial frictions:** information asymmetries and related problems that prevent funds from being allocated efficiently.
* **Financial crisis:** a major disruption of financial markets, typically marked by sharp falls in asset prices and widespread business failures.

### 信贷繁荣和破灭
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Credit Booms and Busts*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

经济体进行经济创新financial innovation时产生了一类新的贷款品种.
金融自由化financial liberalization指取消对金融市场和金融机构的管制.长期上来讲会促进金融发展提升金融体系资本分配效率.但会造成短期金融机构大规模放贷,即信贷繁荣credit boom.
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Financial innovation can create new types of loans and expand the range of borrowers that financial institutions can serve.

**Financial liberalisation** means removing restrictions on financial markets and institutions. It can support long-run financial development and improve capital allocation, but in the short run it may also encourage rapid lending and a credit boom.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

最终贷款越来越多,贷款损失开始积累.就是坏账开始多起来了.  贷款(资产负债表的资方)相对于负债的价值减少.从而拉低银行和其他金融机构的净值水平.此时银行会削减放款,这一过程叫去杠杆化deleveraging.信贷从繁荣走向暴跌.银行的信息不收集了.金融脆弱性加剧.产出下降.
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
As lending expands, underwriting standards may weaken and loan losses begin to accumulate. The value of banks' assets falls relative to their liabilities, reducing the net worth of banks and other financial institutions. They respond by cutting lending and shrinking leverage—**deleveraging**. The credit boom turns into a contraction; less screening and less credit worsen financial frictions, and output falls.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

### 资产价格繁荣和破灭(和前一个差不多)
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Asset-Price Booms and Busts*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

基础经济价值fundamental economic values 基于对资产未来收入流的现实预期的价值.
资产价格泡沫asset-price bubble事资产价格超过其基础价值的部分.
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
**Fundamental value:** an asset's value based on realistic expectations of its future cash flows.
**Asset-price bubble:** the portion of an asset's market price that exceeds its fundamental value.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

信贷繁荣有可能推动资产价格泡沫,因为用于购买资产的贷款大幅度增加会推高资产价格.
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
A credit boom can fuel an asset-price bubble because more borrowing to buy assets pushes their prices higher.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

泡沫破裂后抵押品价格下降银行要求追加抵押物,如果不追加,则金融机构调低杠杆 .同时泡沫破裂后公司的资产价值下降可是负债是死的,导致净值水平下降,这个时候就和信贷繁荣里面的情况相似,后续去杠杆化,贷款减少,不确定性增加
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
When the bubble bursts, collateral values fall and banks demand additional collateral. If borrowers cannot provide it, financial institutions reduce leverage. Firms' assets also lose value while their nominal liabilities remain fixed, so net worth falls. The result resembles the end of a credit boom: deleveraging, less lending, and greater uncertainty.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

不确定性增加,信息减少了,信息不对称性增加,逆向选择严重, 贷款收缩

#### 1. 抵押贷款市场的金融创新
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*1. Financial Innovations in the Mortgage Market*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

创造了结构化信用产品structured credit products.即基于标的资产的现金流量,被设计成的具有特定风险特征的产品.其中担保债务权益collateralized debt obligation CDO. 特殊目的机构special purpose VehicleSPV将CDO风险分级然后以不同的利率卖给不同风险偏好的人.实现了风险隔离,卖CDO的公司把风险全承担了,出问题找不了商业银行,只能找SPV.但是SPV是空壳公司,风险在这一级被隔离下来了,没有缓冲的空间.
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Structured credit products were designed from the cash flows of underlying assets to offer different risk profiles. A prominent example is the collateralised debt obligation (CDO): a special-purpose vehicle (SPV) divides the cash-flow risk into tranches and sells them to investors with different risk preferences and required returns. This structure legally separates the assets from the originating bank, but a thinly capitalised SPV may provide little capacity to absorb losses once the underlying assets deteriorate.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

#### 2.抵押贷款市场的代理问题
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*2. Agency Problems in the Mortgage Market*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

银行发放贷款的时候不太考虑风险等级了,因为银行的风险被SPV隔离了,出现了委托-代理问题.经纪人只想多放贷款,不关注贷款者的信用等级.
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Once loans could be transferred into SPVs, originators had less incentive to bear their long-run credit risk. This created a principal–agent problem: mortgage brokers were rewarded for producing more loans rather than for ensuring that borrowers were creditworthy.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

此时CDO的购买者发现了这些问题,所以他们买保险,信用违约互换credit default swap,在债券违约时为债券的持有者提供偿付的金融衍生工具.这玩意虽然是保险的类型但是名义上是表外业务所以没有被严格监管.这个其实不赚的,但是卖保险的只抽成交的订单的提成,无所谓公司的盈亏.
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
CDO investors sought protection through **credit default swaps (CDS)**, derivatives that promise a payment when a referenced bond defaults. Although economically similar to insurance, CDS were treated as derivatives and were not subject to the same insurance regulation. The people arranging or selling the contracts could earn fees immediately even when the institution ultimately bore large losses.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

#### 3.信息不对称和信用评级机构
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*3. Information Asymmetry and Credit-Rating Agencies*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

评级机构又咨询又评级,赚两份钱.信用评级被严重高估.他们从中赚取巨额的佣金,所以会将信用等级评的非常高.
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Rating agencies could be paid both to advise on deal structures and to rate the resulting securities, creating a conflict of interest. Because issuers paid substantial fees, agencies had incentives to award ratings that were too favourable and understated credit risk.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

(3) A sharp fall in the value of collateral and other financial assets triggered a run on the shadow banking system. This system consisted of hedge funds, investment banks, and other non-depository financial firms that were regulated less strictly than banks. Funds supplied by shadow banks had circulated through the financial system for years, supporting low-interest-rate mortgage and automobile lending.

Much of the funding used to buy these securities came from repurchase agreements (repos): short-term borrowing collateralized by assets such as mortgage-backed securities. As concern about the quality of financial institutions' balance sheets grew, lenders demanded larger amounts of collateral—a higher haircut. For example, to borrow $100 million through a repo with a 5% haircut, a borrower might need to pledge $105 million of mortgage-backed securities.

(4) Global financial markets. Although the problems originated in the United States, the first alarm came from Europe, illustrating how globalized financial markets had become. On 7 August 2007, Fitch and Standard & Poor's downgraded more than $10 billion of mortgage-backed securities and CDOs. BNP Paribas then suspended redemptions from several of its money-market funds after they suffered large losses. A run on the shadow banking system had begun and continued to intensify.
