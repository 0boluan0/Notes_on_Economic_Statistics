# Lessmann et al. (2015): English annotation

> [!cite] Reference
> Lessmann, S., Baesens, B., Seow, H.-V., & Thomas, L. C. (2015). Benchmarking state-of-the-art classification algorithms for credit scoring: An update of research. *European Journal of Operational Research, 247*(1), 124–136. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2015.05.030

## English annotation

Lessmann et al. (2015) examine whether advanced classification methods can perform better than logistic regression in credit scoring. The article was published in the *European Journal of Operational Research* and is mainly relevant to researchers and practitioners in credit risk, data analysis and operational research. The authors compare 41 methods using eight real credit-scoring datasets. They use repeated cross-validation, six performance measures and different assumptions about the costs of classification errors. The results show that random forests, neural networks and some ensemble methods generally perform better than logistic regression. However, the ranking changes when different measures and costs are used. This means that the most accurate model may not create the highest business value. A main strength is that the study compares many methods and datasets, so its results are not based on only one setting. One limitation is that the cost analysis is simulated and does not include regulation, interpretability or maintenance costs. I will use this study to discuss whether the gains from complex models are large and stable enough to justify lower interpretability.
