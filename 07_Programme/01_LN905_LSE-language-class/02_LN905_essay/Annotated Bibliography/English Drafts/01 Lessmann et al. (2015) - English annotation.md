# Lessmann et al. (2015): English annotation

> [!cite] Reference
> Lessmann, S., Baesens, B., Seow, H.-V., & Thomas, L. C. (2015). Benchmarking state-of-the-art classification algorithms for credit scoring: An update of research. *European Journal of Operational Research, 247*(1), 124–136. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2015.05.030

## English annotation

Lessmann et al. (2015), writing for researchers and practitioners in credit scoring, data analytics and operational research, examine whether advanced classification algorithms consistently outperform logistic regression, a standard credit-scoring method. The authors compare 41 classifiers across eight real credit-scoring datasets, using repeated cross-validation, six performance measures and varying misclassification-cost assumptions. They find that random forests, neural networks and several ensemble methods generally outperform logistic regression. However, model rankings change with the evaluation metric and cost structure, meaning that the statistically most accurate classifier is not necessarily the most commercially valuable. A major strength of the study is the breadth of its benchmark across datasets, methods, metrics and cost scenarios, which reduces dependence on a single experimental setting. Nevertheless, the cost analysis is simulated and excludes interpretability, regulation, model maintenance and data drift. The study therefore cannot establish that the best-performing classifier should automatically be deployed. I will use this article as the principal empirical basis for judging whether predictive gains are material and stable, and contrast it with Rudin’s (2019) argument that high-stakes decisions should favour inherently interpretable models.
