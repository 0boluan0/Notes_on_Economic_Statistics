# Rudin (2019): English annotation

> [!cite] Reference
> Rudin, C. (2019). Stop explaining black box machine learning models for high stakes decisions and use interpretable models instead. *Nature Machine Intelligence, 1*, 206–215. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-019-0048-x

## English annotation

Rudin (2019) argues that black-box models should not be used automatically in high-stakes decisions just because they may have higher accuracy. The article was published in *Nature Machine Intelligence* and is relevant to machine-learning researchers, practitioners and policymakers. Rudin explains the difference between an interpretable model and a post-hoc explanation of a black box. Using examples from credit scoring, medicine and criminal justice, she argues that post-hoc explanations may not show how the original model actually makes its decision. They can therefore be misleading or open to manipulation. A main strength of the article is that it clearly questions the idea that accuracy and interpretability always have to be traded against each other. However, it is mainly a theoretical argument and does not directly compare different models using consumer credit data. Therefore, it cannot measure the exact performance loss from choosing an interpretable model. I will use this article to argue that small increases in accuracy are not enough to justify lower transparency, and to evaluate the benefits reported by Lessmann et al. (2015) and Khandani et al. (2010).
