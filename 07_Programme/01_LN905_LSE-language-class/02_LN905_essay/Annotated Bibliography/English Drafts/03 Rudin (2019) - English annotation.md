# Rudin (2019): English annotation

> [!cite] Reference
> Rudin, C. (2019). Stop explaining black box machine learning models for high stakes decisions and use interpretable models instead. *Nature Machine Intelligence, 1*, 206–215. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-019-0048-x

## English annotation

Rudin (2019), an interpretable-machine-learning researcher writing for researchers, practitioners and policymakers, argues that high-stakes decision-makers should not deploy black-box models and then rely on post-hoc methods to explain their predictions. She distinguishes inherently interpretable models from post-hoc explanations and uses examples from credit scoring, medicine and criminal justice to show that an explanation is only an approximation of the original model and may therefore be unfaithful, misleading or open to manipulation. Her central claim is that there is no universal and unavoidable trade-off between accuracy and interpretability. Before adopting a black box, decision-makers should demonstrate both that its predictive gains are material and that no similarly accurate interpretable alternative is available. The article’s main strength is its clear analysis of explanation fidelity and the burden of proof it establishes for high-stakes model selection. However, it is a theoretical and position-based article rather than a controlled empirical comparison focused specifically on consumer credit scoring, so it cannot quantify performance differences between models on particular credit datasets. I will use this article as the principal theoretical challenge to sacrificing transparency for small accuracy gains and to qualify the predictive and economic benefits reported by Lessmann et al. (2015) and Khandani et al. (2010).
