# 1.7.6 A inj B

## Official exercise

For finite sets \(A\) and \(B\), \(\;A \text{ inj } B\) is equivalent to:

Exercise 1

[ ] There exists an injective relation from \(A \) to \(B \)

[ ] There exists a total injective relation from \(A \) to \( B\)

[ ] There exists an injective function from \(A \) to \(B \)

[ ] There exists a surjective relation from \(B \) to \(A \)

[ ] There exists a total surjective relation from \(B \) to \(A \)

[ ] There exists a surjective function from \(B \) to \(A \)

[ ] There exists a bijection from \(A \) to some subset of \(B \)

[ ] There exists a bijection from \(B \) to some subset of \(A \)

[ ] \(\mid A \mid\, \le\, \mid B \mid \)

[ ] \(\mid A \mid\, >\, \mid B \mid \)

[ ] \(\mid A \mid\, =\, \mid B \mid \)

## Official answers and feedback

> [!answer]- Q1 — official answer
> **Answer:** There exists a total injective relation from \(A \) to \( B\); There exists a surjective function from \(B \) to \(A \); There exists a bijection from \(A \) to some subset of \(B \); \(\mid A \mid\, \le\, \mid B \mid \)
>
> **Official feedback:**
> An injective function from \(A \) to \(B \) or a surjective relation from \(B \) to \(A \) do not guarantee \(A \text{ inj } B\).
