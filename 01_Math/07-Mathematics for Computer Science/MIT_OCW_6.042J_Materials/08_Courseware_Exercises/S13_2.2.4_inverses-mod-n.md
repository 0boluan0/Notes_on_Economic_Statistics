## Official exercise

1. Why cannot we cancel the \(2\)'s in the equation \(8\cdot 2 \equiv 3 \cdot 2 (\text{mod } 10)\) to \(8 \equiv 3 (\text{mod } 10)\)?

   Exercise 1

   [Choose one: 2 is a prime number | 2 is even | 2 has common factors with 10 | 10 is not a power of 2 | We CAN cancel the 2]
  
2. Fill in the blank!

   If \(k\) and \(n\) are \_\_\_\_\_, then \(k\) has a multiplicative inverse in the equation \(a\cdot k \equiv b\cdot k (\text{mod } n)\).

   Exercise 2

   [Choose one: collinear | of different parity | relatively prime | invertible | irreducible | distinct]

## Official answers and feedback

> [!answer]- Q1 — official answer
> **Answer:** 2 has common factors with 10

> [!answer]- Q2 — official answer
