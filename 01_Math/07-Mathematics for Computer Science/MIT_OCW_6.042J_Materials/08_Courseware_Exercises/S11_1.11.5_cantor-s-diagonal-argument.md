## Official exercise

1. The Diagonal Argument was used to illustrate that the set of infinite binary sequences, \(\{ 0, 1 \} ^ \omega \), is \_\_\_\_\_,

   Exercise 1

   [Choose one: countable | uncountable | neither countable nor uncountable]

   by building a \_\_\_\_\_

   Exercise 2

   [Choose one: finite binary sequence | infinite binary sequence | Either of the above | Neither of the above]

   that is \_\_\_\_\_ the list of infinite binary sequences.

   Exercise 3

   [Choose one: in | not in]
2. The set of natural numbers is strictly smaller than the set, \(A \), of infinite binary strings (i.e. \(\mathbb{N} \) strict \(A \)), because \_\_\_\_\_.

   Exercise 4

   [Choose one: there is no surjection from the set of natural numbers to \(A \) | there is a bijection from the set of natural numbers to \(A \) | the statement is false! The natural numbers are as large as \(A \) | there is no injective relation from the set of natural numbers to \(A \)]
3. Cantor's Theorem states that for any set \(A \), finite or infinite, \_\_\_\_\_ pow(\(A \)).

   Exercise 5

   [Choose one: \(A \) inj | \(A \) bij | \(A \) surj | \(A \) strict]

## Official answers and feedback

> [!answer]- Q1 — official answer
> **Answer:** uncountable

> [!answer]- Q2 — official answer
> **Answer:** infinite binary sequence

> [!answer]- Q3 — official answer
> **Answer:** not in

> [!answer]- Q4 — official answer
> **Answer:** there is no surjection from the set of natural numbers to \(A \)

> [!answer]- Q5 — official answer
> **Answer:** \(A \) strict
