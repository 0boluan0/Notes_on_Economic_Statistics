## Official exercise

We toss \(n\) fair coins and count the number of heads. Let \(X\_i\) be an indicator variable for the \(i^{th}\) coin.
Let \(A\_n=\dfrac{\sum\_{i=1}^nX\_i}{n}\).

Please format fractional answers as x/y.

1. What is \(E[A\_n]\)?

2. What is the smallest \(n\) can be to guarantee \(\Pr[|A\_n-E[A\_n]|>\frac{1}{10}]\leq \frac{1}{25}\)?

   Exercise 2

   [response]
  
3. What is the smallest \(\delta\) if \(\Pr[|A\_{100}-E[A\_{100}]|>\delta]\leq \frac{1}{4}\)

   Exercise 3

   [response]

## Official answers and feedback

> [!answer]- Q1 — official answer
> **Answer:** 1/2
> **Tolerance:** 0.0001
>
> **Official feedback:**
> By linearity of expectation, \(E[A\_n]=\frac{1}{n}\cdot\sum\_{i=1}^nE[X\_i]=\frac{1}{2}\).

> [!answer]- Q2 — official answer
> **Answer:** 625
> **Tolerance:** 0.0001
>
> **Official feedback:**
> Using Chebyshev's Theorem, with \(\delta=\frac{1}{10}\)
> and \(\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{4n}\), we get \(\Pr[|A\_n-E[A\_n]|>\frac{1}{10}]\leq \frac{25}{n}\),
> so if \(n\geq 625\) the bound holds.

> [!answer]- Q3 — official answer
> **Answer:** 1/10
> **Tolerance:** 0.0001
>
> **Official feedback:**
> We are given that \(n=100\), and from Chebyshev's theorem we get:
> \[\frac{1}{4}=\frac{\frac{1}{4n}}{\delta^2}.\]
> Solving for \(\delta\), we get \(\delta = \frac{1}{10}\).
