# 3.2.4 Little oh Big Oh

> [!info] 来源说明
> 题目、正确答案与反馈均从 MIT OCW 官方离线课程包提取；动态作答控件已转换为静态 Markdown。

## Official exercise

1. Which of the following is true about the little oh and Big Oh?

   Exercise 1

   [ ] If \(f = o(g) \), then \(f = O(g) \).

   [ ] If \(f = o(g) \), then \(g \neq O(f) \).

   [ ] When \(f \sim g \) and \(f \neq o(g) \), \(f = O(g) \).

   [ ] When \(f \nsim g \) and \(f = o(g) \), \(f = O(g) \).

   [ ] If \(f = O(g) \), then \(f = o(g) \).
2. Which of the following is NOT correct about the little oh?

   Exercise 2

   [ ] \(x^{a} = o(x^{b}) \) for all integers \(a \) and \(b \)

   [ ] \(b \cdot x^{a} = o(x^{b}) \) for all nonnegative constants \(b > a\)

   [ ] \(ln(x)=o(x^{\varepsilon}) \) for all \(\varepsilon > 0 \)

   [ ] \(x^{c} = o(a^{x}) \) for any \(a, c \in \mathbb{R} \) with \(a > 1 \)

   [ ] \(log(x)=o(x^{\epsilon}) \) for all \(\epsilon > 0 \)
3. Let \(f(n) = O(g(n)) \). Why do we say \(\limsup\limits\_{x \rightarrow \infty} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \) < \(\infty \) instead of \(lim\_{x \rightarrow \infty} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \) < \(\infty \)?

   Exercise 3

   [ ] It accommodates cases in which a limit of \(f(x)/g(x) \) does not exist.

   [ ] The limit superior, \(limsup \), is a more strict limit compared to the ordinary limit, \(lim \).

   [ ] \(O(g(x)) \) gives an upper bound instead of a lower bound on the growth of \(f(n) \).

   [ ] \(f(x) \) can oscillate wildly as long as it never gets bigger than \(c \cdot g(x) \) for \(x > n \) and \(c > 0 \).

## Official answers and feedback

> [!answer]- Q1 — official answer
> **Answer:** If \(f = o(g) \), then \(f = O(g) \).; If \(f = o(g) \), then \(g \neq O(f) \).; When \(f \sim g \) and \(f \neq o(g) \), \(f = O(g) \).; When \(f \nsim g \) and \(f = o(g) \), \(f = O(g) \).

> [!answer]- Q2 — official answer
> **Answer:** \(x^{a} = o(x^{b}) \) for all integers \(a \) and \(b \)

> [!answer]- Q3 — official answer
> **Answer:** It accommodates cases in which a limit of \(f(x)/g(x) \) does not exist.; \(f(x) \) can oscillate wildly as long as it never gets bigger than \(c \cdot g(x) \) for \(x > n \) and \(c > 0 \).
