## Official exercise

1. On which side of the equal sign should the asymptotic notations \(O() \), \(o() \), and \(\Theta() \) be used?

   Exercise 1

   ( )  left

   ( )  right

   ( )  either
2. Why is the statement "\(f \) is at least \(O(n^2) \)" a blunder?

   Exercise 2

   [ ] \(O(n^2) \) is not a quantity.

   [ ] Big Oh gives an upper bound on the growth of a function.

   [ ] \(O(n^2) \) is a relation.

   [ ] If \(f \) is at least \(n^2 \), then \(n^2 = O(f) \).
3. Why is the statement \(\sum\_{i=1}^{n} i = O(n) \) a blunder?

   Exercise 3

   [ ] \(\sum\_{i=1}^{n} i = o(n) \)

   [ ] The Big Oh relation on individual terms of a sum does not necessarily apply to the sum.

   [ ] \(\sum\_{i=1}^{n} i = \Theta(n^{2}) \)

   [ ] \(O(1) \) should not be treated as a number.
