## 官方原文

### How to structure your weekly studies in ST445

Every teaching week follows the same three steps: prepare, attend, review. Keeping to this rhythm from Week 1 is the most reliable way to do well in the module, because each week builds on the one before.

A typical teaching week, drawn to scale: about 6 hours in total.

| Prepare | Lecture | Seminar | Review |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30–50 min | 2 hours | 1.5 hours | about 2 hours |

### The weekly routine

#### Step 1 — Before class: prepare

30–50 minutes before the lecture or seminar

Complete all pre-class activities on Moodle before your synchronous session. Read assigned materials, watch prep videos, and attempt any short exercises.

#### Step 2 — In class: attend the lecture and seminar

About 3.5 hours of live sessions each week

Attend both synchronous sessions as scheduled on Student Hub (Lecture and Seminar). Bring questions from your pre-class work. Engage actively — these sessions are where core concepts come to life.

#### Step 3 — After class: review and practise

About 2 hours after the live sessions

Complete the post-class activities on Moodle immediately after your session or the following day while material is fresh. These reinforce and extend what was covered.

Dedicate a quiet block before the new week starts to review everything covered this week. Identify gaps, revisit tricky concepts, and prepare mentally for the following Week.

**Reading week (Week 6).** There are no live sessions. Take the self-check quiz on Weeks 1 to 5, catch up on any post-class activities you missed, and confirm that your project dataset can be collected end to end.

### Your project plan, Weeks 7 to 11

From Week 7, add project work to your routine. Setting aside time every week week, in addition to the steps above, keeps the project on track without a last-minute rush.

#### Week 7 — Get to know the project

Understand what is being asked before you start building.

- Read the project brief and the marking rubric.
- Read the documentation for the APIs and libraries you will use.
- Set up your project repository: folder structure, README and environment file.

Done when you have a working repository and a one-page plan of your analysis.

Formative Problem Set 2 runs Monday to Friday this week.

#### Week 8 — Collect and preprocess your data

Build the data your analysis will stand on.

- Run your collectors and save the raw data untouched, with a provenance record for each source.
- Join your sources on common keys, checking row counts and reporting unmatched records.
- Build your cleaning pipeline with validation checks, and record every decision in your cleaning log.

Done when one script turns your raw data into an analysis-ready dataset.

Problem Set 2 opens at 12:00 on Monday. Plan time for both this week.

#### Week 9 — Explore your data

Find out what your data can and cannot tell you.

- Work through the EDA protocol on your dataset.
- Write each finding as an insight statement: what you observed, the evidence, and its limits.
- Draft your main figures following the best practices.

Done when you have a set of evidenced insights and draft figures.

Problem Set 2 is due at 12:00 on Monday of this week.

#### Week 10 — Write your report

Turn your analysis into a clear argument for a named audience.

- Draft each section: collection and integration, preprocessing, exploration, and your recommendation.
- Write your AI-use statement from your journal.

Done when you have a complete first draft.

#### Week 11 — Verify everything

Check that your work runs, reads well and meets the brief.

- Clone your repository into a fresh folder and run it end to end from the raw data.
- Check that your README and environment file let someone else reproduce your results.
- Check that every figure follows best practices, and that every section aligns with the marking rubric.
- Proofread, then submit. Aim to finish before the deadline rather than on it.

Done when your project is submitted.

### Project deadline

Submit your GitHub repository, report and AI-use statement by 12:00 on the Monday immediately after Week 11.

Falling behind? Come to office hours or post on the module forum. It is easier to catch up after one week than after three.
