# 公理是起点而定理引理推论按证明后的角色命名
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*An axiom is a starting assumption, while theorem, lemma, and corollary name roles played by proved propositions*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 原子区别
> **公理**是在某个形式系统中接受为起点的命题。**定理、引理和推论**都已经得到证明：定理通常指重要结论，引理为后续论证提供支点，推论则从已有结果经过很短的推导得到。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> An **axiom** is accepted as a starting proposition within a specified formal system. A **theorem**, **lemma**, and **corollary** have all been proved: a theorem is usually a result of central interest, a lemma supports a later argument, and a corollary follows from an established result in only a few steps.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

这些名字说明结论在一段数学叙述中的角色，不形成严格的价值等级。今天为主定理服务的 lemma，后来可能成为整个领域最常用的结果。阅读证明时，真正要问的是：它是否已被证明、允许依赖哪些前提、此处怎样使用；不能因为标题写着 theorem 就跳过依赖。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
These names describe a result's role in a mathematical exposition rather than a strict ranking of value. A lemma introduced to support one theorem may later become the most widely used result in a field. When reading a proof, ask whether the result has been established, which assumptions it depends on, and how it is used here; the label “theorem” does not eliminate those dependencies.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 边界
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Boundary*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

- “公理”总是相对于一个形式系统或当前约定而言；换系统后，同一命题可能成为可证明定理、额外假设或不成立的陈述。
- 定义规定术语含义，不因被称作“定义”就是真假命题；证明通常从定义展开，但不是在证明定义本身为真。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->

**1.** “Axiom” is relative to a formal system or an explicitly adopted framework. In another system, the same sentence may be a theorem, an additional assumption, or false.<br>
**2.** A definition stipulates meaning and is not automatically a truth-valued proposition merely because it is called a definition. Proofs unfold definitions; they do not normally prove a definition true.<br>
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 回忆与应用
> 某篇论文先证明一个结果，再只用两行从它推出另一个结论。第二个结论为什么适合叫 corollary，而不是 axiom？
>
> **答案：** 它依赖已经证明的结果并由推理得到，不是未经证明接受的起点；“推论”描述的正是这种很短的派生关系。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> A paper proves one result and then derives another in two lines. Why is the second result naturally a corollary rather than an axiom?
>
> **Answer:** It is derived from an established result rather than accepted without proof as a starting point; “corollary” names precisely this short derivation.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
