# 高斯积分在全实线上收敛且积分值为圆周率的平方根
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*The Gaussian integral of the exponential of minus x squared converges on the real line and equals the square root of pi*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

高斯积分是 [[反常积分]] $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2}\,dx$；它的两侧尾积分都收敛，且
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The Gaussian integral is the [[反常积分|improper integral]] $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2}\,dx$. Both one-sided tails converge, and
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$$
\boxed{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2}\,dx=\sqrt\pi},
\qquad \int_0^\infty e^{-x^2}\,dx=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}.
$$

证明把曲线下的面积平方变成曲面 $z=e^{-(x^2+y^2)}$ 下的体积，再利用旋转对称性计算。先在有限区域操作并夹逼极限，就能同时证明收敛和数值，无须预先假设任何概率密度已经归一化。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The proof turns the square of an area into a volume below $z=e^{-(x^2+y^2)}$, then uses rotational symmetry. Working first on finite regions and squeezing the limit proves both convergence and the value, without presupposing that a probability density is normalised.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 有限方形与圆盘夹逼
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Squeezing between finite squares and disks*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

取 $R>0$，令 $Q_R=\int_{-R}^R e^{-x^2}\,dx$。在方形 $B_R=[-R,R]^2$ 上，被积函数连续，有限区域上的逐次积分／[[切片体积]] 给出
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For $R>0$, set $Q_R=\int_{-R}^R e^{-x^2}\,dx$. The integrand is continuous on the square $B_R=[-R,R]^2$, so iterated integration on this finite rectangle, or [[切片体积|volume by slices]], gives
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$$
\iint_{B_R}e^{-(x^2+y^2)}\,dx\,dy
=\int_{-R}^R e^{-y^2}\!\left(\int_{-R}^R e^{-x^2}\,dx\right)dy
=Q_R^2.
$$

令 $D_R$ 为半径 $R$ 的圆盘。圆盘上方的旋转体用 [[圆柱壳法]] 计算：壳半径为 $r$、周长为 $2\pi r$、高度为 $e^{-r^2}$，故
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Let $D_R$ be the disk of radius $R$. Compute the volume above it by [[圆柱壳法|cylindrical shells]]: radius $r$, circumference $2\pi r$, and height $e^{-r^2}$. Thus
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$$
V_R=\iint_{D_R}e^{-(x^2+y^2)}\,dx\,dy
=\int_0^R2\pi r e^{-r^2}\,dr
=\pi(1-e^{-R^2}).
$$

几何上有 $D_R\subset B_R\subset D_{\sqrt2R}$。被积函数非负，扩大底面只会增加体积，因此
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Geometrically, $D_R\subset B_R\subset D_{\sqrt2R}$. The integrand is nonnegative, so enlarging the base can only increase the volume. Therefore,
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$$
\pi(1-e^{-R^2})\le Q_R^2\le\pi(1-e^{-2R^2}).
$$

当 $R\to\infty$，两端都趋于 $\pi$；又 $Q_R>0$，所以 $Q_R\to\sqrt\pi$。由偶性，$\int_0^R e^{-x^2}\,dx=Q_R/2$，两侧尾积分分别有有限极限，故得到通常意义下的反常积分收敛，而不只是对称截断极限。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
As $R\to\infty$, both bounds tend to $\pi$. Since $Q_R>0$, it follows that $Q_R\to\sqrt\pi$. Evenness gives $\int_0^R e^{-x^2}\,dx=Q_R/2$, so both one-sided integrals have finite limits. This establishes ordinary improper convergence, beyond merely a symmetric truncation limit.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

上述换序只用于连续函数在有限方形上的积分；非负性用于区域包含后的大小比较。若改写为全平面的无限重积分，可用非负可测函数的 Tonelli 定理处理换序；有限值由这里的夹逼计算建立。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The interchange above uses continuity on a finite square; nonnegativity justifies comparison under region inclusion. If the proof is written as an integral over the whole plane, Tonelli's theorem permits interchange for a nonnegative measurable integrand. The squeeze calculation establishes that the resulting value is finite.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 归一化常数由积分值推出
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Deriving the normalising constant from the integral*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对 $\alpha>0$，用 $u=\sqrt\alpha x$ 换元，得 $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha x^2}\,dx=\sqrt{\pi/\alpha}$。特别地 $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2/2}\,dx=\sqrt{2\pi}$，因此 $\phi(x)=e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ 非负且总积分为 $1$，满足 [[概率密度函数]] 的归一化要求。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For $\alpha>0$, substituting $u=\sqrt\alpha x$ gives $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha x^2}\,dx=\sqrt{\pi/\alpha}$. In particular, $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2/2}\,dx=\sqrt{2\pi}$. Hence $\phi(x)=e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ is nonnegative and integrates to $1$, meeting the normalisation requirement for a [[概率密度函数|probability density]].
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 如何归一化 $c e^{-4x^2}$，使它成为全实线上的概率密度？为什么平方后取正根？
>
> **答案：** 积分为 $c\sqrt\pi/2$，所以 $c=2/\sqrt\pi$；原积分严格为正，故只取正平方根。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> What value of $c$ normalises $c e^{-4x^2}$ on the real line, and why does the squared-integral argument use the positive root? **Answer:** Its integral is $c\sqrt\pi/2$, so $c=2/\sqrt\pi$. The original integral is strictly positive, selecting the positive square root.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
