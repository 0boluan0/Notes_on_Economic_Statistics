# Supremum
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

*上确界*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Consider all real numbers strictly between $0$ and $1$, the set $A=(0,1)$. Every member is at most $1$, so $1$ is an **upper bound**. The number $2$ is also an upper bound, but it leaves unnecessary room above the set. The **supremum** is the smallest upper bound: here it is $1$. It describes the tight upper boundary even though $1$ itself is not a member of $A$.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

考虑所有严格位于 $0$ 与 $1$ 之间的实数，即集合 $A=(0,1)$。每个成员都不超过 $1$，所以 $1$ 是一个**上界**。$2$ 也是上界，但在集合上方留出了不必要的余量。**上确界**就是最小的上界：这里是 $1$。它描述最紧的上边界，即使 $1$ 本身不属于 $A$。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
## An upper bound that cannot be lowered
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

*不能再降低的上界*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Write $A\subseteq\mathbb R$ to say that $A$ is a set of real numbers. A real number $u$ is an upper bound when $a\le u$ for **every** $a\in A$. If at least one such $u$ exists, $A$ is bounded above. For a nonempty set $A$, a real number $s$ is its supremum, written $s=\sup A$, when both conditions hold:
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

写 $A\subseteq\mathbb R$ 表示 $A$ 是实数集合。若**每个** $a\in A$ 都满足 $a\le u$，实数 $u$ 就是上界；若至少存在一个这样的 $u$，就说 $A$ 有上界。对于非空集合 $A$，实数 $s$ 是它的上确界，记作 $s=\sup A$，需要同时满足：

$$\begin{aligned}
&a\le s &&\text{for every }a\in A,\\
&s\le u &&\text{for every upper bound }u\text{ of }A.
\end{aligned}$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The first line says that $s$ really bounds the set. The second compares $s$ with **all other upper bounds**, not only with members of $A$. These conditions give uniqueness: if $s$ and $t$ both satisfy them, then $s\le t$ and $t\le s$, so $s=t$. The [[实数的确界性质|least-upper-bound property of the real numbers]] guarantees existence when $A$ is nonempty and bounded above; existence is an additional property of the number system, not part of what the word “supremum” means.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

第一行说明 $s$ 确实界住了集合。第二行把 $s$ 与**所有其他上界**比较，而不仅仅与 $A$ 的成员比较。这两个条件保证唯一性：若 $s,t$ 都满足，就有 $s\le t$ 且 $t\le s$，因此 $s=t$。[[实数的确界性质]]保证非空、有上界的实数集合存在这样的数；存在保证是数系额外具备的性质，并非“上确界”这个词的定义本身。

> [!example] Supremum and maximum · 上确界与最大值
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> A **maximum** must be a member of the set and at least as large as every other member. The set $(0,1)$ has supremum $1$ but no maximum: for any $a\in(0,1)$, the number $(a+1)/2$ is still in the set and is larger than $a$. In contrast, $[0,1]$, which includes both endpoints, has maximum and supremum both equal to $1$. Thus a finite supremum is a maximum exactly when it belongs to the set.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> **最大值**必须属于集合，并且不小于其他任何成员。$(0,1)$ 的上确界是 $1$，但它没有最大值：任取 $a\in(0,1)$，$(a+1)/2$ 仍在集合里，而且比 $a$ 大。相比之下，包含两个端点的 $[0,1]$ 的最大值和上确界都是 $1$。所以，有限上确界恰在属于集合时才是最大值。

## Approaching the upper boundary
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

*任意接近上边界*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
There is a useful way to test whether an upper bound is the least one. A finite real number $s$ is $\sup A$ exactly when it is an upper bound and, for every positive tolerance $\varepsilon$, some member of $A$ lies within $\varepsilon$ below it:
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

检验一个上界是否最小，可以看集合能否任意接近它。有限实数 $s$ 是 $\sup A$，当且仅当它是上界，并且对每个正的容差 $\varepsilon$，都能在它下方不足 $\varepsilon$ 的范围内找到集合成员：

$$\forall\varepsilon>0\quad\exists a\in A:\quad s-\varepsilon<a\le s.$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Why? If $s=\sup A$ but no such $a$ existed, $s-\varepsilon$ would be a smaller upper bound. Conversely, suppose the displayed approximation holds and $u<s$. Choosing $\varepsilon=s-u$ gives a member $a>u$, so $u$ cannot be an upper bound. Together with the requirement that $s$ itself is an upper bound, this proves it is the least one.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

为什么？若 $s=\sup A$，却没有这样的 $a$，那么 $s-\varepsilon$ 就成了更小的上界，矛盾。反过来，若上式成立且 $u<s$，选 $\varepsilon=s-u$，就能找到成员 $a>u$，所以 $u$ 不可能是上界。再结合 $s$ 本身是上界，就证明它最小。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For $(0,1)$, $0.9$, $0.99$ and $0.999$ illustrate the approach, but finitely many examples alone do not establish “every $\varepsilon$”. Given any $\varepsilon>0$, take $a=1-\min\{\varepsilon/2,1/2\}$, where $\min$ means the smaller of the two numbers. Then $0<a<1$ and $1-\varepsilon<a$. The chosen $a$ may change with $\varepsilon$; there need not be one member that works for all tolerances. If the supremum is attained, choosing $a=s$ works for every tolerance.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对 $(0,1)$，$0.9,0.99,0.999$ 展示了接近过程，但有限几个例子不能证明“每个 $\varepsilon$”。给定任意 $\varepsilon>0$，取 $a=1-\min\{\varepsilon/2,1/2\}$，其中 $\min$ 表示两者中较小的数，就有 $0<a<1$ 且 $1-\varepsilon<a$。所选的 $a$ 可以随 $\varepsilon$ 改变，不必有一个成员适用于所有容差。若上确界已取到，始终选 $a=s$ 即可。

## The lower boundary by reflection
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

*用反射理解下确界*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
A **lower bound** $l$ satisfies $l\le a$ for every $a\in A$. The greatest lower bound is the **infimum**, written $\inf A$. Reflect the set through zero by forming $-A=\{-a:a\in A\}$. Reflection reverses inequalities, so for a nonempty set bounded below,
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

**下界** $l$ 满足对每个 $a\in A$ 都有 $l\le a$；最大的下界叫**下确界**，记作 $\inf A$。把集合关于零反射，得到 $-A=\{-a:a\in A\}$。反射使不等号反向，所以对非空、有下界的集合，

$$\inf A=-\sup(-A).$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For example, $(0,1)$ has infimum $0$ but no minimum. This card uses finite real bounds. An unbounded-above set has no real supremum; writing $\sup A=+\infty$ uses an extended-real convention, not an additional real member or maximum.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如，$(0,1)$ 的下确界是 $0$，但没有最小值。本卡讨论有限实数界。没有上界的集合不存在实数上确界；写 $\sup A=+\infty$ 是扩展实数约定，并不是额外找到了一个实数成员或最大值。
