# 线性近似是在基点匹配函数值和导数的仿射函数
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*A linear approximation is the affine function matching the value and derivative at the base point*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 它是什么
> 若 $f$ 在 $a$ 可导，则它在 $a$ 的线性近似是
> $$
> L_a(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a),
> $$
> 并且
> $$
> f(a+h)=f(a)+f'(a)h+o(h)\qquad(h\to0).
> $$
> 后一式才是“曲线在足够小范围内像切线”的精确含义：误差除以 $|h|$ 后趋于零。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> If $f$ is differentiable at $a$, its linearization is $L_a(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)$, and $f(a+h)=f(a)+f'(a)h+o(h)$. The little-o statement makes the tangent approximation precise: the error divided by $|h|$ tends to zero.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

构造时先选一个函数值和导数容易计算、又靠近目标输入的基点 $a$。例如 $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$ 在 $a=4$ 有 $f(4)=2$、$f'(4)=1/4$，所以
$$
\sqrt{4.1}\approx 2+\frac14(0.1)=2.025.
$$
近似式必须连同基点一起使用；把 $\ln(1+x)\approx x$ 从 $x\approx0$ 搬到 $x=100$，不是“精度稍差”，而是离开了这条局部陈述的适用范围。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Choose a base point at which the function and derivative are easy to evaluate and which is close to the target input. For $\sqrt{x}$ at $a=4$, the linearization gives $\sqrt{4.1}\approx2.025$. The base point is part of the approximation: applying $\ln(1+x)\approx x$ at $x=100$ abandons the local claim rather than merely reducing its precision a little.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

线性近似只匹配函数值和一阶导数。若多个近似式相乘，可以先展开，再只保留总次数不超过一的项；孤零零保留某个二次项会伪装成二次近似，却没有匹配完整的二阶信息。要给数值误差证书，还需 [[导数界控制函数差]] 或 [[Taylor余项]]，不能只凭“看起来很近”。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
A linearization matches only the value and first derivative. When combining local expansions, retain all and only the terms of total degree at most one. Keeping an isolated quadratic term would imitate a second-order approximation without matching the full second-order information. A numerical error certificate requires a derivative bound or a Taylor remainder, not visual closeness alone.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 为什么 $f(a+h)-L_a(a+h)=o(h)$ 比“切线很接近曲线”更有内容？
>
> **答案：** 它指定了误差相对于步长的消失速度，并因此区分了一阶主项与更小余项；“很接近”既没有尺度，也没有极限条件。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> **Check:** What does $f(a+h)-L_a(a+h)=o(h)$ tell us that “the tangent is close to the curve” does not?
>
> **Answer:** It specifies how the error vanishes relative to the step size, distinguishing the first-order term from a smaller remainder. “Close” supplies neither a scale nor a limiting condition.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
