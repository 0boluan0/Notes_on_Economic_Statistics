# 一元函数的微分是导数作用于输入增量所得的一阶线性输出变化
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*The differential of a one-variable function is the first-order linear output change obtained by applying the derivative to an input increment*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 它是什么
> 对 $y=f(x)$，在固定基点 $x$ 任选输入微分 $dx$，定义
> $$
> dy=df_x(dx)=f'(x)\,dx.
> $$
> $dy$ 是导数这个线性映射预测的一阶输出变化；真实有限变化则是 $\Delta y=f(x+\Delta x)-f(x)$。取 $dx=\Delta x$ 时，可导性给 $\Delta y=dy+o(dx)$。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> For $y=f(x)$ at a fixed base point, choose an input differential $dx$ and define $dy=df_x(dx)=f'(x)dx$. This is the first-order output predicted by the derivative as a linear map. The actual finite change is $\Delta y=f(x+\Delta x)-f(x)$, and when $dx=\Delta x$, differentiability gives $\Delta y=dy+o(dx)$.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如 $y=x^2$、$x=3$、$dx=0.01$ 时，$dy=2x\,dx=0.06$；真实变化是
$$
\Delta y=(3.01)^2-9=0.0601.
$$
两者差 $(dx)^2$，正是被线性模型丢掉的二阶项。把 $dy$ 与 $\Delta y$ 写成等号，会抹掉近似误差。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For $y=x^2$ at $x=3$ with $dx=0.01$, the differential is $0.06$, while the actual change is $0.0601$. Their difference is the discarded quadratic term. Writing $dy=\Delta y$ would erase the approximation error.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

若 $y=f(u)$、$u=g(x)$，则
$$
dy=f'(u)\,du,\qquad du=g'(x)\,dx,
$$
合起来就是 [[链式法则]]。Leibniz 记号允许有纪律地组织变换，但不是允许把所有符号无条件当普通分数约掉；每一步仍要保留函数关系、基点和定义域。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For a composition, $dy=f'(u)du$ and $du=g'(x)dx$ combine into the chain rule. Leibniz notation organizes the transformation, but it does not make every differential expression an ordinary fraction that may be cancelled without regard to functions, base points, or domains.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> $dx$ 与 $\Delta x$ 取同一个数时，为什么仍不是同一概念？
>
> **答案：** $\Delta x$ 指原函数输入的真实有限变化；$dx$ 是送入导数线性映射的增量。数值可同，所生成的 $dy$ 与真实 $\Delta y$ 通常只近似相等。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> **Check:** If $dx$ and $\Delta x$ are assigned the same number, why do they still represent different concepts?
>
> **Answer:** The quantity $\Delta x$ is an actual finite change in the function's input; $dx$ is the increment supplied to the derivative as a linear map. Their numerical values may coincide, while the predicted $dy$ and actual $\Delta y$ generally agree only approximately.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
