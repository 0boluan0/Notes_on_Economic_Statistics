# 不变资本是以生产资料形式存在并在生产中转移已有价值的资本部分
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Constant capital exists as means of production and transfers existing value during production*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

不变资本（constant capital）是资本以原料、辅助材料、机器等生产资料形式存在的部分。在 Marx 的价值分析中，这些资料进入生产时，把自身已有价值按正常耗用的程度转移到产品中；这部分资本在所考察的生产过程中不创造超过自身已有价值的增量。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Constant capital is the part of capital existing as means of production, including raw materials, auxiliary materials and machinery. In Marx's analysis of value, these means transfer their existing value to the product to the extent that they are normally used up. They do not create an increment beyond their own existing value within the production process under consideration.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

原料通常在一次生产中耗用，机器则可以连续使用多年，逐次把耗损的价值转移到产品。假设一台机器价值1000单位，正常使用1000天且每天均匀耗损，那么当天转移1单位；整台机器参与当天生产，产品当天却只承接这一份耗损价值。生产资料的物质作用与价值转移额由此可以不同。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Raw materials are normally used up in a production operation, whereas a machine can serve for years and transfer its value gradually through wear. Suppose a machine is worth 1,000 value units and normally lasts 1,000 days with uniform daily wear. It transfers one unit on that day. The whole machine participates in production, while only that day's wear enters the product's value. Its physical contribution and the amount of value transferred are therefore distinct.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

这一转移通过活劳动的具体、有用形式完成：纺纱使棉花和纺锭耗损的价值保存在纱中。同一活劳动又以抽象劳动的性质创造新价值，因而需同时区分 [[具体劳动]]、[[抽象劳动]] 与 [[可变资本]]。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Value is transferred through the concrete, useful form of living labour: spinning preserves the value of cotton and spindle wear in yarn. The same living labour creates new value in its character as abstract labour. This requires distinguishing [[具体劳动|concrete labour]], [[抽象劳动|abstract labour]] and [[可变资本|variable capital]].
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

“不变”限定的是价值增殖中的作用。若生产棉花的社会条件改变，棉花价值可以升降；若制造机器所需劳动减少，现存机器也可能贬值。这些变化发生于相关生产资料的再生产条件，不改变它们在当前生产中作为不变资本的身份。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
“Constant” specifies the part played in valorisation. Cotton can rise or fall in value when its social conditions of production change; an existing machine can lose value when less labour is needed to manufacture such machines. These changes arise from the conditions of reproducing the means of production and do not alter their status as constant capital in the current process.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

Volume I, Chapter 8 supports the definition, value transfer through consumption, whole-machine participation with gradual transfer, and the boundary illustrated by cotton and machinery revaluation. The MIA text uses the Moore and Aveling translation edited by Engels, based on the 1887 first English edition with indicated fourth German edition changes. The 1,000-day example follows the chapter's figures.
