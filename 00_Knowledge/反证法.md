# 反证法必须由目标的否定导出依赖该反设的矛盾
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*A proof by contradiction must derive, from the negated target, a contradiction that depends on that assumption*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 原子程序
> 要证明命题 $P$，先假设 $\neg P$，再与定义、公理或已证结果一起推出某个不可能同时成立的结论。只有当矛盾确实用到了 $\neg P$，才说明这个反设不能成立，从而得到 $P$。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> To prove a proposition $P$, assume $\neg P$ and combine that assumption with definitions, axioms, or established results to derive an impossibility. Only when the contradiction genuinely depends on $\neg P$ does it show that the assumption is untenable and hence that $P$ holds.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

证明 $\sqrt 2$ 无理时，反设它可写成最简分数 $n/d$。推理会迫使 $n$ 与 $d$ 都是偶数，这与“最简”冲突。冲突来自反设所引入的最简分数，因此能否定反设。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
To prove that $\sqrt 2$ is irrational, assume it can be written as a fraction $n/d$ in lowest terms. The argument forces both $n$ and $d$ to be even, contradicting the lowest-terms condition. Because the contradiction arises from the representation introduced by the assumption, it defeats that assumption.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 边界
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Boundary*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

- 单独写出一个与反设无关的错误等式，不会证明目标；必须标明矛盾怎样由反设与可靠事实共同产生。
- 对蕴含 $P\Rightarrow Q$ 做逆否证明，是直接证明等价命题 $\neg Q\Rightarrow\neg P$；它不要求导出矛盾，因此不要与反证法混写。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->

**1.** An erroneous equation unrelated to the assumption proves nothing. The argument must show how the contradiction follows from the assumption together with sound facts.<br>
**2.** A contrapositive proof of $P\Rightarrow Q$ directly proves the equivalent implication $\neg Q\Rightarrow\neg P$. It need not derive a contradiction and should not be conflated with contradiction proof.<br>
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 回忆与应用
> 某论证反设 $P$ 为假，随后在完全没有使用这个反设的情况下推出 $0=1$。它是否已经证明了 $P$？
>
> **答案：** 没有。这个矛盾没有依赖 $\neg P$，只说明后续推理或其他前提有问题；它不能排除 $\neg P$。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> An argument assumes that $P$ is false and then derives $0=1$ without ever using that assumption. Has it proved $P$?
>
> **Answer:** No. Because the contradiction does not depend on $\neg P$, it only exposes an error in the later reasoning or in some other premise; it does not rule out $\neg P$.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
