# 债券应计利息是按合约日计数和付息归属规则计算的期间利息调整额
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Bond accrued interest is the period-interest adjustment determined by day-count and coupon-entitlement conventions*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

债券应计利息（accrued interest，$AI$）是在两次付息日之间，按适用的日计数和息票归属规则确定的利息调整额。普通含息交易中，买方将取得完整的下次息票，因此在净价之外补偿卖方已经持有期间对应的利息。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Accrued interest, $AI$, is the interest adjustment between coupon dates calculated under the applicable day-count and coupon-entitlement rules. In an ordinary cum-coupon trade, the buyer receives the next full coupon and compensates the seller, in addition to clean price, for the seller's accrued portion.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对规则付息、买方有权取得下一息票 $C$ 的交易，可写 $AI=C\alpha$，其中 $\alpha$ 是按指定惯例计算的已过付息期比例。Actual/Actual 情形可用“已过实际天数／本期实际天数”；其他债券可能使用 30/360 等规则，不能把所有债券都固定除以 $365$。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For a regular coupon period in which the buyer receives the next coupon $C$, write $AI=C\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the elapsed fraction under the stated convention. Actual/Actual can use elapsed actual days divided by actual days in the period; other bonds may use conventions such as 30/360. A universal division by 365 is inappropriate.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如每期息票 $3$、本期 $180$ 天、已过 $60$ 天，采用这一 Actual/Actual 规则时 $AI=3\times60/180=1$。这不是投资者额外赚到一笔免费利息，而是交易双方对整期息票的结算分配。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For a coupon of 3, a 180-day period, and 60 elapsed days under that Actual/Actual rule, $AI=3\times60/180=1$. This is a settlement allocation of the full-period coupon, not an additional free interest payment.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

应计利息也不一定为正。例如英国普通国债采用 DMO 的除息规则：若卖方保留下次息票、交易在该除息期内结算，标准息期的调整为 $AI=C(\alpha-1)<0$，称为 rebate interest。这个负应计规则属于明确的市场惯例，不能无条件推广到任意债券。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Accrued interest need not be positive. Under DMO's ex-dividend convention for conventional UK gilts, the seller retains the next coupon when settlement is in the ex-dividend period. For a standard period, the adjustment is $AI=C(\alpha-1)<0$, called rebate interest. This is a specific market convention, not an unconditional rule for every bond.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
