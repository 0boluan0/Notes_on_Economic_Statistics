# 半正定矩阵的二次型在向量上为零当且仅当该向量属于矩阵的零空间
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*For a positive-semidefinite matrix, a vector has zero quadratic-form value exactly when it lies in the matrix nullspace*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 核心等价
> 若实对称 $A\succeq0$，则对任意 $x\in\mathbb R^n$，
> $$
> x^TAx=0\iff Ax=0.
> $$
> 若 $A$ 是复 Hermitian 半正定矩阵，则对应结论是 $x^*Ax=0\iff Ax=0$。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> For a real symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix, $x^TAx=0$ exactly when $Ax=0$. For a complex Hermitian positive-semidefinite matrix, replace $x^T$ by $x^*$.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

把 $A$ 写成谱分解 $A=Q\Lambda Q^T$，并令 $y=Q^Tx$。因为每个 $\lambda_i\ge0$，
$$
x^TAx=\sum_i\lambda_i y_i^2=0
$$
只能在每个正特征值方向的坐标都为零时发生。于是 $\Lambda y=0$，从而 $Ax=Q\Lambda y=0$。反向更直接：若 $Ax=0$，则 $x^TAx=0$。复数情形把 $y_i^2$ 换成 $|y_i|^2$。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
In spectral coordinates, the quadratic form is a sum of nonnegative terms. A zero sum forces every coordinate associated with a positive eigenvalue to vanish, hence $Ax=0$. The converse follows immediately from $Ax=0$. In the Hermitian case the terms are $\lambda_i|y_i|^2$.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}\succeq0,
\qquad
x=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}.
$$
这里 $x^TAx=0$，而且 $Ax=0$。零二次型不是“正负项碰巧抵消”；半正定性保证它只能来自真正的零空间方向。若矩阵不是半正定，这个结论会失败，例如 $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ 在 $x=(1,1)^T$ 上二次型为零，却有 $Ax\ne0$。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Here $x^TAx=0$ and $Ax=0$. The zero quadratic value is not an accidental cancellation of positive and negative terms: positive semidefiniteness forces it to come from a genuine nullspace direction. Without positive semidefiniteness the conclusion fails; for example, $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ has zero quadratic value at $x=(1,1)^T$ even though $Ax\ne0$.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 若 $A\succeq0$ 且 $N(A)=\{0\}$，为什么 $A$ 必正定？
>
> **答案：** 任意非零 $x$ 都不在零空间中，因此不可能有 $x^*Ax=0$；半正定又排除了负值，所以必有 $x^*Ax>0$。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> If $A\succeq0$ and $N(A)=\{0\}$, why must $A$ be positive definite?
>
> **Answer:** No nonzero $x$ lies in the nullspace, so $x^*Ax$ cannot be zero. Positive semidefiniteness also rules out negative values, hence $x^*Ax>0$ for every nonzero $x$.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
