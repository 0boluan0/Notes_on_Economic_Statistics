# Smith 认为长期只重复少数简单操作可能提高专门熟练却使更广泛的判断能力缺少锻炼

一个动作做得越来越熟，并不意味着面对其他问题时也越来越会思考。Smith 讨论 [[分工]] 时指出：如果一个人的日常工作长期局限于少数重复操作，几乎不需要判断新情况或寻找解决办法，那么他的专门熟练可以提高，更广泛的理解和判断能力却可能因缺少运用而萎缩。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Becoming more proficient at one operation need not improve a person's ability to think through other problems. Smith argues that when [[分工|division of labour]] confines daily work to a few repetitive operations requiring little judgment or invention, specialised dexterity may improve while broader understanding and judgment weaken through lack of exercise.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

这与 [[分工增产机制]] 并不矛盾。工场一天生产多少，和一个劳动者能否理解陌生问题、参与公共事务，衡量的是不同结果。前一个结果改善，不能单独保证后一个结果也改善。Smith 担心的正是：整个社会的职业越来越多样，某个劳动者一生接触的工作反而越来越单一。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
This does not contradict [[分工增产机制|the productivity mechanisms of division of labour]]. A workshop's daily output and a worker's ability to understand unfamiliar problems or public affairs are different outcomes. Improvement in the former does not establish improvement in the latter. Smith is concerned that the range of occupations across society can expand while an individual's working experience becomes increasingly narrow.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

他因此为普通人的基本教育提出公共支持：阅读、书写和算术可以在开始工作以前获得，地方学校和较低的学习费用能够减轻家庭负担。他的理由不只是教育以后能多赚多少钱，也包括避免理解能力长期失去锻炼。这个回应承认分工带来的生产收益，同时要求关注劳动者的能力发展。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
He therefore argues for public support for basic education. Reading, writing and arithmetic can be learned before working life, and local schools with modest fees can reduce the burden on families. The concern is not only future earnings but also preventing a lasting lack of intellectual exercise. The response recognises productive gains while attending to workers' wider capabilities.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

用现代 [[人力资本]] 的语言看，专门操作技能只是个人能力的一部分；这有助于理解两种结果为何不同，但不是说 Smith 已经提出某个现代人力资本增长模型。同样，这段历史论证提出的是值得关注的机制，不能不考察实际工作内容、培训和教育机会，就断言所有现代专业岗位都会使人失去判断力。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
In modern [[人力资本|human-capital]] terminology, proficiency at a specialised operation is only one component of personal capability. That distinction helps explain the two outcomes without attributing a modern human-capital growth model to Smith. His historical argument identifies a mechanism to examine, not grounds for assuming that every modern specialised job diminishes judgment regardless of its tasks, training or educational opportunities.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

Book V, Chapter I, Part III, Article II, Econlib paragraphs 177–185 supply the argument: paragraph 178 concerns repetitive work, 179 contrasts individual and social variety, 181 concerns workers' time constraints, and 182–185 discuss basic education. The existing human-capital definition supplies a modern comparison, not a model attributed to Smith.
