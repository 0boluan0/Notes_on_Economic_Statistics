# 平均边际效应把观测特定效应按给定样本或权重分布平均，并不自动等于总体因果效应

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*An average marginal effect averages observation-specific effects over a stated sample or weighting distribution; it is not automatically a population causal effect*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 平均的是哪群人
> 令 $m_i$ 为第 $i$ 个观测处的连续边际效应或离散变化。无权样本 AME 是
> $$
> \widehat{AME}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n m_i,
> $$
> 有分析或抽样权重时则按预先定义的归一化权重平均。换一个样本、目标总体或权重分布，AME 可以改变，即使同一组模型系数不变。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> Let $m_i$ denote the continuous marginal effect or discrete change at observation $i$. The unweighted sample AME is $\widehat{AME}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n m_i$; with analytical or sampling weights, it is averaged using prespecified normalized weights. Changing the sample, target population, or weighting distribution can change the AME even when the fitted model coefficients stay the same.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

AME 与“在平均协变量处的边际效应”（MEM）不同。AME 先在每个真实协变量配置上算效应再平均；MEM 先把协变量平均，再为这个可能不存在的“平均人”算一次效应。对非线性模型，两者一般不相等。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
An AME differs from a marginal effect at the means (MEM). An AME first computes the effect at every observed covariate configuration and then averages those effects. An MEM first averages the covariates and then computes one effect for that potentially nonexistent “average person.” In a nonlinear model, the two are generally unequal.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如同一 logit 系数在概率曲线中部产生较大概率变化，在两端产生较小变化。若样本 A 多在中部、样本 B 多在尾部，两者的 AME 会不同。这不是计算冲突，而是平均分布不同。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For example, the same logit coefficient produces a larger probability change near the middle of the probability curve and a smaller change in its tails. If sample A is concentrated near the middle and sample B near the tails, their AMEs will differ. This is not a computational inconsistency; the averaging distributions differ.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

样本 AME 首先是模型依赖的描述性汇总。只有当样本和权重代表目标总体、对比对应明确干预且识别假设成立时，它才可能估计某个总体平均因果效应。不能从“average”一词自动推出总体代表性或因果性；标准误也必须计入 $\hat\beta$ 带来的估计不确定性。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
A sample AME is first of all a model-dependent descriptive summary. It can estimate a population average causal effect only if the sample and weights represent the target population, the contrast corresponds to a well-defined intervention, and the identification assumptions hold. The word “average” does not by itself imply population representativeness or causality. Its standard error must also incorporate the estimation uncertainty in $\hat\beta$.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
