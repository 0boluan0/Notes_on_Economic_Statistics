# 直接证明假设前件并用定义与已知结果推出后件
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*A direct proof assumes the antecedent and derives the consequent from definitions and established results*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 原子程序
> 要直接证明 $P\Rightarrow Q$，先假设 $P$，再只用该假设、明示定义和已经允许使用的结论推到 $Q$。不能在中途把 $Q$ 或与 $Q$ 等价的内容当作已知。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> To prove $P\Rightarrow Q$ directly, assume $P$ and derive $Q$ using only that assumption, explicit definitions, and established results available in the context. Do not assume $Q$, or an equivalent form of $Q$, along the way.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如，取任意奇整数 $n$。由定义，存在 $k\in\mathbb Z$ 使 $n=2k+1$。于是
$$
n^2=4k^2+4k+1=2(2k^2+2k)+1,
$$
所以 $n^2$ 能写成 $2$ 乘整数再加 $1$，按定义也是奇数。这个证明的主干是“任意满足前件的对象 → 展开前件定义 → 到达后件定义”。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For example, take an arbitrary odd integer $n$. By definition, $n=2k+1$ for some $k\in\mathbb Z$. Then
$$
n^2=4k^2+4k+1=2(2k^2+2k)+1,
$$
so $n^2$ is twice an integer plus one and is therefore odd. The backbone is “arbitrary object satisfying the antecedent → unfold its definition → reach the definition of the consequent.”
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 边界
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Boundary*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

- 从目标倒推可以用于草稿探索，但正式证明必须确认每一步可逆，或改写成从前提向结论的有效推导。
- 直接证明不等于“只做代数”。文字必须说明对象、量词、定义和每个关键变形为何成立。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->

**1.** Backward reasoning from the goal can be useful scratchwork, but a final proof must verify that each backward step is reversible or rewrite the argument as valid reasoning from premises to conclusion.<br>
**2.** A direct proof is not merely algebra. Its prose should identify the objects, quantifiers, definitions, and justification for each decisive transformation.<br>
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 回忆与应用
> 要直接证明“若 $n^2$ 偶，则 $n$ 偶”，为什么不能开头就写 $n=2k$？
>
> **答案：** $n=2k$ 正是后件“$n$ 偶”的定义，开头这样写等于预设待证结论。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> In a direct proof of “if $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even,” why may one not begin with $n=2k$?
>
> **Answer:** $n=2k$ is precisely the definition of the desired consequent, so beginning there assumes what must be proved.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
