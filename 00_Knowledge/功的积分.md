# 一维变力做功通过积分带符号的力乘位移来计算
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Work by a variable force in one dimension is the integral of signed force against displacement*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

沿一条固定直线选定正向坐标 $x$，令 $F(x)$ 是所研究的力沿正向的带符号分量。物体沿该直线从 $x=a$ 到 $x=b$，且该力只依赖位置、在所经区间可积时，它所做的功为
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Choose a positive coordinate direction along a fixed line, and let $F(x)$ be the signed component of the force being studied. For motion from $x=a$ to $x=b$, if the force depends only on position and is integrable along the interval traversed, its work is
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$$
W=\int_a^b F(x)\,dx.
$$

小位移的功约为 $F(x_i^*)\Delta x_i$，积分把它们累加。力与位移同向时贡献为正、反向时为负；积分上下限保留运动方向，不能为了让结果为正就加绝对值。SI 单位为牛顿乘米，即焦耳。若力另有时间或运动方向依赖，应沿实际运动使用 $W=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}F(t)x'(t)\,dt$；不能仅凭起终点套用同一个位置函数。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Work over a small displacement is approximately $F(x_i^*)\Delta x_i$. Aligned force and displacement contribute positively; opposite directions contribute negatively. The bounds preserve the direction of motion, and absolute values must not be inserted merely to make the answer positive. SI units are newton-metres, or joules. If force also depends on time or direction of motion, use $W=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}F(t)x'(t)\,dt$ along the actual trajectory.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

弹簧例：以自然长度位置为 $x=0$，理想弹簧的回复力为 $-kx$，其中 $k>0$。从伸长 $a$ 移到伸长 $b$ 时，弹簧自身做功为 $-\frac{k}{2}(b^2-a^2)$；若外力缓慢拉伸并抵消回复力，外力做功才是 $\frac{k}{2}(b^2-a^2)$。在 $0\le a<b$ 的拉伸中，二者一负一正。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For an ideal spring, let $x=0$ denote natural length and let its restoring force be $-kx$, with $k>0$. Moving from extension $a$ to $b$, the spring does work $-k(b^2-a^2)/2$. An external force that slowly balances the spring instead does $k(b^2-a^2)/2$. For $0\le a<b$, the spring's work is negative and the external work is positive.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

提水例：质量密度为 $\rho$ 的水平水片在高度 $y$ 处面积为 $A(y)$，被缓慢提升到出口高度 $H$。在忽略损耗和动能变化、且 $H\ge y$ 时，所需外力功为 $dW=\rho g A(y)(H-y)\,dy$；再对所有水片积分。这里 $dy$ 标记不同水片，$H-y$ 才是每片移动的距离；若给的是重量密度，就不再乘 $g$。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
A horizontal water layer of mass density $\rho$ and area $A(y)$ at height $y$ is slowly lifted to outlet height $H$. Neglecting losses and changes in kinetic energy, with $H\ge y$, the external work is $dW=\rho gA(y)(H-y)\,dy$. Integrate over the layers. Here $dy$ labels different layers, while $H-y$ is each layer's lifting distance. Weight density already includes the gravitational factor.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
