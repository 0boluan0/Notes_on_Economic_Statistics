# 微分方程在右端未定义的奇点不约束跨点延拓并可使两侧解常数彼此独立
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*A singular point where an ODE's right-hand side is undefined does not constrain continuation across the point and may leave independent constants on the two sides*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 原子边界
> 若标准形 $y'=F(x,y)$ 在某点或曲线上没有定义，方程就不能在该处提供斜率。两侧解通常属于不同的连通区间；即使某个拼接函数在奇点处有值或导数，也不能把它称为在包含该点的区间上满足原方程的解。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> If $F(x,y)$ in $y'=F(x,y)$ is undefined at a point or along a curve, the equation supplies no slope there. Solutions on the two sides normally live on separate connected intervals. Even if a patched function has a value or derivative at the singularity, it does not satisfy the original ODE on an interval containing a point where that ODE is undefined.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

方程
$$
y'=\frac{2y}{x}
$$
在 $x\ne0$ 的解为 $y=Ax^2$。左半轴与右半轴可以分别选择 $A_-$、$A_+$；把两支都补成 $y(0)=0$ 甚至会得到在零点可导的函数，但原方程在零点仍无定义，所以这个点没有把两个常数锁成一个。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For $y'=2y/x$, solutions on each side of zero are $Ax^2$. The constants on the negative and positive half-lines may be chosen independently. Filling in $y(0)=0$ can even make the patched function differentiable there, but the ODE remains undefined at zero and imposes no relation between the two constants.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

这与“正则方程在同一点有多个解”的非唯一性要区分。这里首先是方程本身没有覆盖该点；正确做法是分别报告两侧最大区间，而不是把奇点处的任意赋值包装成一个常规初值问题。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
This differs from nonuniqueness for a regular ODE whose right-hand side is defined at the initial point. Here the equation itself does not cover the point. Report the separate maximal intervals rather than presenting an arbitrary fill-in as an ordinary initial value problem.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 若拼接函数在 $x=0$ 可导，为什么仍不能说它在全实线上解 $y'=2y/x$？
>
> **答案：** 方程右侧在 $x=0$ 无定义，无法在全实线逐点成立；函数自身可导不能补上方程缺失的定义。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> **Check:** If the patched function is differentiable at zero, why is it still not a solution of $y'=2y/x$ on the whole real line?
>
> **Answer:** The equation's right-hand side is undefined at zero, so the equation cannot hold pointwise on the whole line. Differentiability of the function does not supply the equation's missing definition.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
