# Improper integral
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
*反常积分*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
An **improper integral** asks whether accumulation has a finite total when the interval keeps extending, or when the function becomes unbounded near a point. We first calculate ordinary integrals on finite, valid pieces. We then ask whether their values approach one finite number as the pieces reach farther or approach the problematic point.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

**反常积分**问的是：区间不断延长，或者函数在某点附近变得无界时，累积量能否仍有一个有限总值？先在有限、合法的小范围内计算普通积分，再看范围不断扩大或靠近问题点时，这些积分值是否趋于同一个有限数。

> [!example] An endless interval can have a finite total · 无限长的区间也能有有限总量
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> Imagine a decreasing [[自然指数函数|exponential]] rate $f(x)=e^{-x}$ starting at $x=0$. It is positive, with base $e\approx2.71828$. Up to a finite time $T>0$, its [[定积分|definite integral]] is
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 设从 $x=0$ 开始，一个递减的[[自然指数函数|指数速率]]为 $f(x)=e^{-x}$。它始终为正，底数 $e\approx2.71828$。到有限时刻 $T>0$ 为止，其[[定积分]]是
>
> $$A(T)=\int_0^T e^{-x}\,dx=[-e^{-x}]_0^T=1-e^{-T}.$$
>
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> The [[Newton-Leibniz公式|endpoint formula]] gives the calculation because $(-e^{-x})'=e^{-x}$ and the functions are continuous on every such interval. As $T$ grows, $e^{-T}$ tends to zero, so $A(T)$ tends to $1$. No finite $T$ gives all of that total: the amount still missing is $e^{-T}$. We define $\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,dx=1$ through this limit.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 因为 $(-e^{-x})'=e^{-x}$，且相应函数在每个这样的区间上连续，可以用 [[Newton-Leibniz公式|端点公式]]计算。$T$ 不断增大时，$e^{-T}$ 趋于零，$A(T)$ 因而趋于 $1$。任何有限 $T$ 都尚未累积到全部总量，仍差 $e^{-T}$。我们通过这个极限，定义 $\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,dx=1$。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
## A finite integral must come first
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
*先保证每个有限截断都有普通意义*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Let $a$ be a finite real number and let $f:[a,\infty)\to\mathbb R$. Assume that $f$ is **Riemann integrable on every finite interval $[a,T]$ with $T>a$**. This is the local proper-integrability requirement: before taking any new limit, the ordinary integrals inside it must exist. [[连续函数可积|Continuity on each such interval]] is a sufficient condition, but the definition only needs integrability.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

设 $a$ 为有限实数，$f:[a,\infty)\to\mathbb R$。要求 $f$ **在每个有限区间 $[a,T]$（$T>a$）上都黎曼可积**。这就是这里所需的局部正常可积条件：取新的极限之前，里面的普通积分必须先存在。[[连续函数可积|在每个这样的区间上连续]]是一个充分条件，但定义本身只要求可积。

> [!important] Convergence means a finite real limit · 收敛指有限实数极限
>
> $$\int_a^\infty f(x)\,dx:=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\int_a^T f(x)\,dx.$$
>
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> If this limit exists as a finite real number $L$, the improper integral **converges** and its value is $L$. Otherwise it **diverges**. The symbol $T$ remains a finite real input while the limit is tested; $\infty$ is a direction of approach. See [[无穷极限与无穷远极限|limits at infinity]] for the precise meaning of approaching $L$ for every sufficiently large $T$.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 若此极限存在且为有限实数 $L$，就称反常积分**收敛**，其值为 $L$；否则称为**发散**。检查极限时，$T$ 始终是有限实数输入，$\infty$ 表示趋近方向。[[无穷极限与无穷远极限|无穷远极限]]中，“趋于 $L$”要求所有充分大的 $T$ 都满足相应精度。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For a left infinite interval, use $\int_{-\infty}^b f=\lim_{A\to-\infty}\int_A^b f$, assuming every finite truncation is proper. Renaming a cutoff does not change the definition. If an interior singularity prevents proper integrability on those finite intervals, split at that point before applying the one-end definition.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对于左端无限的区间，使用 $\int_{-\infty}^b f=\lim_{A\to-\infty}\int_A^b f$，同样要求每个有限截断都正常可积。截断参数改名不会改变定义。若内部奇点使这些有限区间不再正常可积，须先在该点拆开，再应用单端定义。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
## A finite endpoint can also require a limit
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
*有限端点也可能需要极限*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For finite $a<b$, suppose $f$ is defined on $(a,b]$ and is Riemann integrable on every $[a+\varepsilon,b]$ with $0<\varepsilon<b-a$. A possible singularity at $a$ is kept outside each truncated interval:
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

设 $a<b$ 均有限，$f$ 定义在 $(a,b]$ 上，并在每个 $[a+\varepsilon,b]$ 上黎曼可积，其中 $0<\varepsilon<b-a$。每次截断都把 $a$ 处可能的奇点留在区间之外：

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx:=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\int_{a+\varepsilon}^b f(x)\,dx.$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The notation $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ means a positive cutoff distance tending to zero. No value of $f(a)$ is needed. For a problem at the right endpoint, use $\lim_{t\uparrow b}\int_a^t f$, with proper integrability on every $[a,t]$. Each definition again requires a finite limiting value to count as convergence.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$\varepsilon\downarrow0$ 表示正的截断距离趋于零，不需要使用 $f(a)$ 的值。若问题在右端点，则用 $\lim_{t\uparrow b}\int_a^t f$，并要求每个 $[a,t]$ 上都正常可积。两种定义都仍然要求极限有限，才称为收敛。

> [!example] Unbounded height, finite area · 高度无界，面积有限
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> For $f(x)=1/\sqrt{x}$ on $(0,1]$, the height grows without bound near zero, but
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 对 $(0,1]$ 上的 $f(x)=1/\sqrt{x}$，零点附近的高度无界，但
>
> $$\int_\varepsilon^1\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}}
> =[2\sqrt{x}]_\varepsilon^1=2-2\sqrt{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow2.$$
>
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> Thus $\int_0^1x^{-1/2}\,dx=2$. Assigning a finite value at zero would not make the function bounded on $[0,1]$; this is genuinely a limit of proper integrals, not a repair obtained by filling in one missing value.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 因此 $\int_0^1x^{-1/2}\,dx=2$。在零点补一个有限值，并不能让函数在 $[0,1]$ 上有界；这里依靠的是正常积分的极限，并不是补上一个缺失函数值就完成了修复。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
## Convergence at every problematic end
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
*每一个问题端都要分别收敛*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
On the whole real line, first assume $f$ is integrable on every finite closed interval. Choose any finite splitting point $c$ and define
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对整条实轴，先假设 $f$ 在每个有限闭区间上可积。任选有限分点 $c$，定义

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f
:=\int_{-\infty}^c f+\int_c^{\infty}f,$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
**only when both terms converge separately**. For an interior singularity, the same requirement applies to its left and right sides. With finitely many problematic points, isolate them and check each one-sided limit; an interval with two problematic ends needs a regular splitting point between them. The precise procedure is in [[反常积分逐坏点拆分|splitting at each improper endpoint]]. These definitions do not allow two divergent terms to cancel. A coordinated symmetric limit is a different object, discussed in [[Cauchy 主值与反常积分|Cauchy principal value versus improper integral]].
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

**只有两项各自收敛，才定义这个和。** 对内部奇点，也要求其左右两侧分别收敛。若问题点有限多个，就将它们分开，逐个检查单侧极限；若某一段的两个端点都有问题，还需在中间选正常分点。具体步骤见[[反常积分逐坏点拆分]]。这些定义不允许两个发散项相互抵消；协调两侧的对称极限是另一个对象，见 [[Cauchy 主值与反常积分]]。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
## Divergence can also be oscillation
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
## “发散”不只表示趋于正无穷

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For nonnegative $f$, the truncated accumulation can only increase, so its failure to have a finite limit is growth to $+\infty$. A function taking both signs can fail differently. For example,
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

若 $f$ 非负，截断累积只能增加，因此没有有限极限时就会趋于 $+\infty$。但函数若有正有负，失败方式可能不同。例如

$$\int_0^T\sin x\,dx=1-\cos T.$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
At $T=2\pi n$ this equals $0$; at $T=(2n+1)\pi$ it equals $2$, for positive integers $n$. Both cutoff sequences go to infinity, so there is no single limit. The truncated values stay bounded, but $\int_0^\infty\sin x\,dx$ still diverges. Checking only a convenient sequence of cutoffs would miss the failure. The required limit is a limit of **integral values**, not simply a limit of the integrand itself.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

取正整数 $n$，在 $T=2\pi n$ 时结果为 $0$；在 $T=(2n+1)\pi$ 时结果为 $2$。两列截断都趋于无穷，因此不存在同一个极限。截断积分值虽然有界，$\int_0^\infty\sin x\,dx$ 仍然发散。只检查一列方便的截断，就会漏掉这个问题。需要检查的是**积分值**的极限，而不只是被积函数本身的极限。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The same finite-limit convention calls growth to $-\infty$ divergence too. An antiderivative may help calculate the finite truncations, but its existence alone does not settle their limit; [[无穷不是积分端点|infinity is not a value to substitute into it]].
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

按同一个有限极限约定，趋于 $-\infty$ 也属于发散。原函数可以帮助计算有限截断，但仅有原函数还不能判断这些值的极限；[[无穷不是积分端点|无穷不是能直接代进去的函数输入]]。

> [!question]- Change the starting point · 改变起点
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> What is $\int_1^\infty e^{-x}\,dx$? First keep $T>1$ finite: $\int_1^T e^{-x}\,dx=e^{-1}-e^{-T}$. The limit is $e^{-1}$, because the initial segment from $0$ to $1$ has been excluded.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> $\int_1^\infty e^{-x}\,dx$ 是多少？先保留有限的 $T>1$，得到 $\int_1^T e^{-x}\,dx=e^{-1}-e^{-T}$；极限为 $e^{-1}$，因为起点之前 $0$ 到 $1$ 的累积被排除了。
