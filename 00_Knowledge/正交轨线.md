# 正交轨线是在与给定曲线族相交处切线互相垂直的曲线
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*An orthogonal trajectory is a curve whose tangent is perpendicular to the given family's tangent at their intersections*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 它是什么
> 给定一个曲线族，一条曲线若在与该族成员的交点处都具有垂直于原曲线的切线，就称为该族的正交轨线。这里在双方切线均有定义的正规交点讨论；用非零切向量 $u,v$ 表达，就是
> $$
> u\cdot v=0.
> $$
> “正交”要求切线方向成直角，不只是两条曲线发生相交。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> A curve is an orthogonal trajectory of a given family if its tangent is perpendicular to the original curve's tangent at their intersections. This concerns regular intersections where both tangents exist. For nonzero tangent vectors $u,v$, the condition is $u\cdot v=0$. Merely intersecting is not enough: the tangent directions must form a right angle.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如，以原点为圆心、半径 $R>0$ 的圆与所有过原点的直线正交。在交点 $(x,y)$，直线的方向是 $(x,y)$，圆的切线方向是 $(-y,x)$，于是
$$
(x,y)\cdot(-y,x)=-xy+xy=0.
$$
这个例子用几何方向直接验证定义，不必先把圆写成单值函数。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Every circle centered at the origin with radius $R>0$ is orthogonal to the family of lines through the origin. At an intersection, the radial direction $(x,y)$ and circular tangent $(-y,x)$ have zero dot product. This verifies the definition directly without forcing the circle into one function graph.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

水平与竖直切线也能互相正交，因此负倒数斜率不是定义本身。双方斜率都有限时，垂直条件可以写成 $m_1m_2=-1$；原斜率为零时，另一条切线是竖直的。如何从曲线族建立斜率方程并求解，见[[正交轨线求解]]；那是使用这一定义的方法。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Horizontal and vertical tangents can be perpendicular, so a reciprocal-slope formula is not the definition itself. If both slopes are finite, orthogonality means $m_1m_2=-1$; a zero original slope instead requires a vertical tangent. The [[正交轨线求解|solution method]] explains how to turn the definition into a slope equation and solve it.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 为什么不能在原曲线斜率为零时直接计算 $-1/m$？
>
> **答案：** 代数分式无定义；几何上正交方向应是竖直线，需要用方向而不是有限斜率表达。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> **Check:** Why can we not directly evaluate $-1/m$ when the original tangent slope is zero?
>
> **Answer:** The fraction is undefined. Geometrically the perpendicular direction is vertical, so it must be represented by a direction rather than a finite slope.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
