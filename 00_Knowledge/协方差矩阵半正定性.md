# 实随机向量的协方差矩阵是对称半正定矩阵，其二次型等于相应线性投影的方差
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*The covariance matrix of a real random vector is symmetric positive semidefinite, and its quadratic form equals the variance of the corresponding linear projection*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 核心结论
> 设随机向量 $X\in\mathbb R^p$ 有有限二阶矩，$\mu=E[X]$，
> $$\Sigma=E[(X-\mu)(X-\mu)^T].$$
> 则 $\Sigma=\Sigma^T$，并且对任意 $a\in\mathbb R^p$，
> $$a^T\Sigma a=\operatorname{Var}(a^TX)\ge0.$$
> 因而 $\Sigma\succeq0$。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> If a real random vector has finite second moments, its covariance matrix is symmetric and satisfies $a^T\Sigma a=\operatorname{Var}(a^TX)\ge0$ for every direction $a$. Hence it is positive semidefinite.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对称性来自 $\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=\operatorname{Cov}(X_j,X_i)$。二次型等式则把矩阵条件翻译成一维事实：任何线性投影的方差都不能为负。沿单位特征向量 $q_i$ 投影时，方差正好是对应特征值 $\lambda_i$。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Symmetry follows from interchangeability of covariance arguments. The quadratic-form identity reduces the matrix condition to the one-dimensional fact that variance cannot be negative. Along a unit eigenvector $q_i$, the projected variance equals the eigenvalue $\lambda_i$.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对复随机向量 $Z$，协方差采用 $\Sigma=E[(Z-\mu)(Z-\mu)^*]$；此时 $\Sigma$ 是 Hermitian 矩阵，并满足 $a^*\Sigma a=E|a^*(Z-\mu)|^2\ge0$。半正定协方差何时出现零方向，见 [[协方差奇异性]]。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For a complex random vector, covariance is defined by $\Sigma=E[(Z-\mu)(Z-\mu)^*]$. It is Hermitian and satisfies $a^*\Sigma a=E|a^*(Z-\mu)|^2\ge0$. [[协方差奇异性|The covariance singularity criterion]] explains exactly when a zero direction occurs.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 为什么只检查每个对角元 $\Sigma_{ii}\ge0$ 还不足以证明一个对称矩阵是合法协方差矩阵？
>
> **答案：** 对角元只检查坐标轴方向的方差；还必须有 $a^T\Sigma a\ge0$ 对每个方向 $a$ 成立。比如 $\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}$ 对角元为正，却在 $a=(1,-1)^T$ 方向给出负二次型，所以不可能是协方差矩阵。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> Why is checking only $\Sigma_{ii}\ge0$ for every diagonal entry insufficient to show that a symmetric matrix is a valid covariance matrix?
>
> **Answer:** Diagonal entries test variances only along the coordinate axes. One also needs $a^T\Sigma a\ge0$ in every direction $a$. For example, $\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}$ has positive diagonal entries but a negative quadratic value in the direction $a=(1,-1)^T$, so it cannot be a covariance matrix.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
