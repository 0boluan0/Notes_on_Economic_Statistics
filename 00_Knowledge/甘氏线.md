# 甘氏线是在指定时间价格单位下从同一起点按固定比率画出的射线

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Gann lines are rays drawn from a common origin at fixed ratios of specified time and price units.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

甘氏线是一组从选定行情起点向未来延伸的固定比率射线，用于提供可能的支撑、阻力和走势快慢参照。本课按“时间×价格”记号：$m\times n$ 表示每经过 $m$ 个时间单位，价格改变 $n$ 个价格单位；因此同一单位设置下，$2\times1$ 比 $1\times1$ 平缓。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Gann lines extend fixed-ratio rays from a selected origin as possible support, resistance and rate-of-movement references. The course uses time × price: $m\times n$ means a change of $n$ price units over $m$ time units. With identical units, $2\times1$ is flatter than $1\times1$.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

固定线性价格坐标，设一个时间单位为 $u_t$、一个价格单位为 $u_P$，且 $m,n,u_t,u_P>0$，起点为 $(t_0,P_0)$，则相应上升或下降线可写为：

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
On a fixed linear price scale, let one time unit be $u_t$, one price unit be $u_P$, with $m,n,u_t,u_P>0$, and the origin be $(t_0,P_0)$. The rising or falling ray is:
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$$
P(t)=P_0\pm\frac{n\,u_P}{m\,u_t}(t-t_0),\qquad t\ge t_0.
$$

若一个时间单位是一交易日、一个价格单位是2元，上升 $2\times1$ 的斜率为每天1元。从100元出发，经过六个交易日后的位置是106元。$1\times1$ 只有在两种基准单位被画成相同屏幕长度时才呈45°；缩放图表会改变视觉角度。改用对数价格坐标须另定义单位，不能无说明沿用此线性价差公式。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
With one trading day per time unit and 2 currency units per price unit, a rising $2\times1$ gains 1 currency unit per day, reaching 106 from 100 after six trading days. A $1\times1$ appears at 45° only when the base units have equal displayed lengths. Rescaling changes the visual angle. Log-price coordinates require their own unit definition rather than silently reusing this linear-price formula.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

不同软件可能倒置记号顺序：TradingView文档的 $2\times1$ 表示每时间单位变动两个价格单位，即价格×时间。因此复用图线必须记录顺序、尺度、方向和起点，不能只抄“2×1”或图上的角度。固定比率是绘图规则，不是价格必然遵循的运动规律。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Software can reverse the notation: TradingView's documented $2\times1$ is two price units per time unit, or price × time. Reuse therefore requires the order, scale, direction and origin, not just the label or visual angle. Fixed ratios are drawing rules, not laws that prices must obey.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
