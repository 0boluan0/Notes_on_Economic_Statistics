# Why infinity is not an integration endpoint value
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
*无穷不是积分端点*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
In $\int_1^\infty x^{-2}\,dx$, the upper symbol tells us to keep moving a finite cutoff to the right. It does not give us an extra real number called infinity to put into a formula. The reliable calculation has two stages: **evaluate a proper finite integral, then examine its limit**. This is the [[反常积分|improper-integral definition]] applied to an antiderivative calculation.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

在 $\int_1^\infty x^{-2}\,dx$ 中，上限符号告诉我们：让有限截断点不断向右移动。它没有提供一个叫作“无穷”的额外实数，让我们代入公式。可靠的计算分两步：**先求正常的有限积分，再检查它的极限**。这就是将[[反常积分]]的定义用于原函数计算。

> [!example] Every substitution is finite · 每次代入的数都有限
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> For a real cutoff $R>1$, the integrand is continuous on $[1,R]$. Its [[原函数|antiderivative]] $F(x)=-1/x$ satisfies $F'(x)=1/x^2$ there, so the [[Newton-Leibniz公式|Newton–Leibniz formula]] gives
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 取实数截断点 $R>1$。被积函数在 $[1,R]$ 连续，[[原函数]] $F(x)=-1/x$ 在这里满足 $F'(x)=1/x^2$，因此由 [[Newton-Leibniz公式]]，
>
> $$\int_1^R\frac{dx}{x^2}
> =F(R)-F(1)=1-\frac1R.$$
>
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> Now let $R\to+\infty$. Since $1/R\to0$, the result tends to $1$:
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 再令 $R\to+\infty$。因为 $1/R\to0$，结果趋于 $1$：
>
> $$\int_1^\infty\frac{dx}{x^2}
> =\lim_{R\to+\infty}\left(1-\frac1R\right)=1.$$
>
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> The unaccumulated tail is $1/R$: after $R=10$ the accumulated value is $0.9$, and after $R=100$ it is $0.99$. These are ordinary numbers approaching the total.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 尚未累积的尾部是 $1/R$：截断到 $R=10$ 时已累积 $0.9$，到 $R=100$ 时为 $0.99$。这些都是普通数，逐渐逼近总值。

## When an endpoint difference is justified
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
*什么时候可以使用端点差*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For finite $a<R$, first check that $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[a,R]$ and that $F$ is continuous there, differentiable inside, with $F'=f$. These hypotheses justify $\int_a^R f=F(R)-F(a)$. A common sufficient setting is a continuous integrand and an antiderivative extending continuously to the finite endpoints. Then
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对有限的 $a<R$，先检查 $f$ 在 $[a,R]$ 上黎曼可积，而 $F$ 在这段上连续、内部可导且满足 $F'=f$。这些条件保证 $\int_a^R f=F(R)-F(a)$。常见的充分情形是：被积函数连续，所用原函数连续延伸到有限端点。此时

$$\int_a^\infty f(x)\,dx
=\lim_{R\to+\infty}\bigl(F(R)-F(a)\bigr)$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
is a convergent improper integral exactly when the displayed limit is finite. As $F(a)$ is a fixed finite number, this is equivalent to $F(R)$ approaching a finite value. A notation such as $F(\infty)$ can be used only as an explicitly defined abbreviation for that limit; it is not evaluation at an element of $F$'s real domain. See [[无穷极限与无穷远极限|limits at infinity]] for the distinction.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

当且仅当这里的极限有限，才得到收敛的反常积分。由于 $F(a)$ 是固定有限数，这也等价于 $F(R)$ 趋于某个有限值。若使用 $F(\infty)$，只能明确将它约定为上述极限的缩写，不能把它当成在实数定义域某个元素处求值。区别见[[无穷极限与无穷远极限]]。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The same care applies at a finite singular endpoint: compute, for example, $F(b)-F(a+\varepsilon)$ before letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Do not subtract values across an interior singularity where the theorem's interval hypotheses fail; [[跨奇点积分拆分|split into valid pieces]] and check their limits separately.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

有限奇异端点也一样：例如先计算 $F(b)-F(a+\varepsilon)$，再令 $\varepsilon\downarrow0$。若内部奇点破坏了定理对整段区间的要求，不能跨过去直接相减；应[[跨奇点积分拆分|拆成合法片段]]，分别检查极限。

## Finding an antiderivative is not a convergence test
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
## 原函数存在也不自动保证收敛

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For $1/x$ on $[1,R]$, the antiderivative is $\ln x$, the [[自然对数|natural logarithm]]. Every finite calculation succeeds:
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对 $[1,R]$ 上的 $1/x$，原函数是[[自然对数]] $\ln x$。每个有限计算都没有问题：

$$\int_1^R\frac{dx}{x}=\ln R-\ln1=\ln R.$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
But $\ln R\to+\infty$, so $\int_1^\infty dx/x$ diverges. By contrast, $1/x^2$ has the finite limit found above. A third possibility is oscillation: $-\cos x$ is an antiderivative of $\sin x$, but $1-\cos R$ has no limit as $R\to\infty$. The [[反常积分#“发散”不只表示趋于正无穷|two cutoff sequences]] show this explicitly. Thus even a bounded, easily computed endpoint difference need not converge.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

但 $\ln R\to+\infty$，所以 $\int_1^\infty dx/x$ 发散；相比之下，$1/x^2$ 得到了前面算出的有限极限。还可能出现振荡：$-\cos x$ 是 $\sin x$ 的原函数，但 $R\to\infty$ 时 $1-\cos R$ 没有极限。[[反常积分#“发散”不只表示趋于正无穷|两列截断值]]具体说明了原因。因此，即使端点差有界而且容易计算，也未必收敛。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
## Keep a finite difference together before taking its limit
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
*先保留有限的差，再取极限*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Writing “$\infty-\infty$” cannot tell us what a difference does. For $x\ge1$, consider the positive function
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

写成“$\infty-\infty$”不能说明一个差究竟如何变化。对 $x\ge1$，考虑正函数

$$f(x)=\frac1x-\frac1{x+1}=\frac1{x(x+1)}.$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
On each finite $[1,R]$, subtraction of the two proper integrals is valid. Using the [[对数函数求导|logarithm derivative]] to find their antiderivatives gives
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

在每个有限 $[1,R]$ 上，可以合法地将两个正常积分相减。由[[对数函数求导]]找到原函数，得到

$$\begin{aligned}
\int_1^R f(x)\,dx
&=\ln R-\bigl(\ln(R+1)-\ln2\bigr)\\
&=\ln\!\left(\frac{2R}{R+1}\right)
\longrightarrow\ln2.
\end{aligned}$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The ratio inside the logarithm tends to $2$, and the logarithm is continuous there. Yet $\int_1^R dx/x$ and $\int_1^R dx/(x+1)$ each tend to $+\infty$. They do not define two convergent improper integrals that can be subtracted. We obtained $\ln2$ by keeping the original finite difference together and then taking its limit. This is not cancellation between divergent contributions from two different ends: those must each converge under the [[反常积分逐坏点拆分|separate-end definition]].
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对数内部的比值趋于 $2$，对数在这里连续。但 $\int_1^R dx/x$ 与 $\int_1^R dx/(x+1)$ 各自都趋于 $+\infty$，并没有构成两个可以相减的收敛反常积分。结果 $\ln2$ 来自保留原来有限的差，再对这个差取极限。这与把两个不同问题端的发散贡献抵消不同；按[[反常积分逐坏点拆分|逐端定义]]，不同问题端必须各自收敛。

> [!question]- What is missing from the shorthand? · 缩写省略了什么？
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> Saying “$e^{-\infty}=0$, so $\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,dx=1$” leaves the definition implicit. Spell it out as $\int_0^R e^{-x}\,dx=1-e^{-R}$ for finite $R>0$, then $\lim_{R\to\infty}(1-e^{-R})=1$. Every evaluation is finite; only the final step is a limit.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> “$e^{-\infty}=0$，所以 $\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,dx=1$”省略了定义步骤。把它写全：先对有限 $R>0$ 计算 $\int_0^R e^{-x}\,dx=1-e^{-R}$，再取 $\lim_{R\to\infty}(1-e^{-R})=1$。每次函数求值都在有限输入处，最后一步才是极限。
