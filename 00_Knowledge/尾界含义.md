# 尾界给出概率上界而不是精确概率
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Tail bounds are upper guarantees, not exact probabilities*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 原子结论
> 对事件 $A$，尾界给出的 $P(A)\le B$ 是一个**保证**：真实概率不会超过 $B$。它既不声称 $P(A)=B$，也不声称真实概率接近 $B$。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> A tail bound of the form $P(A)\le B$ is a **guarantee**: the true probability cannot exceed $B$. It says neither that $P(A)=B$ nor that the true probability is close to $B$.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

同一个不等式可以对某些分布达到等号，对另一些分布却非常宽松。因而“上界更小”只表示保证更强；除非另有 lower bound 或 exact distribution，不能据此判断近似误差。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The same inequality may be attained by some distributions and be extremely loose for others. A smaller upper bound therefore means a stronger guarantee, but it does not quantify approximation error unless a lower bound or the exact distribution is also available.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

若代入后得到 $B>1$，陈述 $P(A)\le B$ 虽然没有逻辑错误，却不如概率公理 $P(A)\le1$。应报告

$$
P(A)\le \min\{1,B\}=1,
$$

并明确称它为 **trivial bound（平凡界）**：现有信息和所选不等式没有排除任何合法概率。不能把 $B=1.4$ 写成“概率是 $140\%$”。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
If substitution gives $B>1$, the statement $P(A)\le B$ is logically true but weaker than the probability axiom $P(A)\le1$. Report $P(A)\le\min\{1,B\}=1$ and call it a **trivial bound**: the available information and chosen inequality have ruled out no admissible probability. A value such as $B=1.4$ is not a probability of $140\%$.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如，知道某事件的真实概率是 $0.003$ 与知道它满足上界 $0.02$ 是两种不同信息；后者只保证“不超过 $0.02$”。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For example, knowing that an event has exact probability $0.003$ is different from knowing only the upper bound $0.02$; the latter guarantees merely that the probability is no greater than $0.02$.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 某次计算得到 $P(A)\le1.3$。它说明了什么？
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> A calculation gives $P(A)\le1.3$. What has been learned?
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> **答案：** 只得到平凡界 $P(A)\le1$；不能报告“概率为 $130\%$”，也不能说真实概率接近 1。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> **Answer:** Only the trivial bound $P(A)\le1$ has been obtained. It is not a probability of $130\%$, nor evidence that the true probability is close to one.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

- Sections 20.1.1 and 20.5.7 of [[01_Math/07-Mathematics for Computer Science/MIT-6-042j-pdf.pdf|MIT Mathematics for Computer Science]] support the distinction between an upper bound and an exact probability, including examples where Markov is exact or very loose and a comparison of polynomial and exponential guarantees.
