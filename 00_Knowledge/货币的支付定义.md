# 货币由普遍接受的支付能力界定而不等于纸币硬币
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Money is defined by general acceptance in payment, not by being notes and coins*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 原子定义
> 货币是能够被广泛接受、用来支付商品与服务并清偿债务的资产或索取权。纸币和硬币只是货币的一种形态；可按面值用于支付的银行存款也可以是货币。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> Money is an asset or claim that is widely accepted for paying for goods and services and settling debts. Notes and coins are only one form of money; bank deposits usable at par for payment can also be money.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

“是不是货币”首先是功能和制度问题，不是材料问题。现金是实体形态，存款是账户上的金融索取权，但两者都可能在各自支付网络内履行货币功能。反过来，一项资产即使容易出售，也不因“值钱”或“可交易”就自动成为普遍接受的支付手段。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Whether something is money is first a functional and institutional question, not a question about its material form. Cash is physical, while a deposit is an account-based financial claim, yet both may perform monetary functions in their payment networks. Conversely, an asset does not become a generally accepted means of payment merely because it is valuable or tradable.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 易错边界

- currency 通常指纸币和硬币；money 的范围可以更广。
- “法定货币”是法律地位；实际支付接受度还取决于支付基础设施、可兑付性和信任。
- 股票、债券和房屋属于财富，但通常不能直接在日常交易中按标价面额完成支付。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
- Currency normally means notes and coins; money can be broader.
- Legal-tender status is a legal rule, while practical acceptance also depends on payment infrastructure, redeemability, and trust.
- Shares, bonds, and houses are wealth, but they are not normally tendered directly at face value in everyday payment.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 银行存款不是纸币，为什么仍可算货币？
>
> **答案：** 因为客户可以用它按面值发起支付或清偿债务；判断标准是被接受的支付能力，而不是是否有实体纸张。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> Why can a bank deposit count as money even though it is not a banknote?
>
> **Answer:** The customer can use it at par to initiate payments or settle debts. The criterion is accepted payment capacity, not physical paper.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
