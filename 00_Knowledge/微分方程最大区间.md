# 微分方程的最大解区间是该解无法继续延拓为同一方程之解的定义区间
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*A maximal solution interval is a domain beyond which the solution cannot be extended as a solution of the same ODE*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 它是什么
> 给定方程 $y'=F(x,y)$，设 $y$ 是区间 $I$ 上的解。若不存在更大的区间 $J\supsetneq I$ 和其上的解 $\widetilde y$，使 $\widetilde y$ 在 $I$ 上与 $y$ 完全相同，就称 $y$ 是最大解，$I$ 是它的最大解区间。这里的“延拓”必须保留已有轨迹，并继续满足同一个方程。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> Let $y$ solve $y'=F(x,y)$ on an interval $I$. If no solution on a strictly larger interval $J$ agrees with $y$ throughout $I$, then $y$ is maximal and $I$ is its maximal solution interval. An extension must preserve the existing trajectory and satisfy the same equation.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

把一个解限制到较小的区间，仍然得到合法的解；只有要求尽可能延拓时，才需要找最大区间。例如 $F$ 在包含初始点的开定义域上为 $C^1$ 时，局部存在唯一性保证同一初值有唯一的最大解；若唯一性条件失效，不能仅凭“最大”二字宣称只有一条轨迹，见 [[通解与初值]]。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Restricting a solution to a smaller interval still gives a valid solution. Seeking the maximal interval asks how far it can be extended. For example, if $F$ is $C^1$ on an open domain containing the initial point, local existence and uniqueness yield a unique maximal solution for those data. Maximality alone does not imply uniqueness.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如 $y'=y^2$、$y(0)=1$ 的解为
$$
y(x)=\frac1{1-x}.
$$
它在 $(-\infty,1)$ 上满足方程，且 $x\to1^-$ 时趋向 $+\infty$，因此不能连续延拓穿过 $1$。在 $(-1/2,1/2)$ 上它也解同一初值问题，只是这段还不是最大区间。若改取初值 $y(2)=-1$，同一公式给出的另一条最大解位于 $(1,\infty)$。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For $y'=y^2$ with $y(0)=1$, the solution $1/(1-x)$ blows up as $x\to1^-$, so its maximal interval is $(-\infty,1)$. Its restriction to $(-1/2,1/2)$ remains a solution of the same initial value problem. With different initial data $y(2)=-1$, the same formula describes a different maximal solution on $(1,\infty)$.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

当前公式的定义域只能提供候选区间。比如 $y'=1$、$y(1)=1$ 的解若暂写成 $y=x^2/x$，表达式在零点无定义，但轨迹可以用 $y=x$ 延拓到整个实线。要判断是否真正到达终点，应回到原方程，检查能否补值、换一种表达或继续一个合法分支；公式出现分母、对数或根号限制，本身还不是无法延拓的证明。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The domain of a written formula is only a candidate. For $y'=1$, $y(1)=1$, the expression $x^2/x$ fails at zero, yet the solution extends as $y=x$ over the entire real line. Check the original equation before treating a denominator, logarithm, or root restriction as an obstruction to extension.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

真正的障碍包括解在有限点爆破、继续后的函数不再满足所需可导性，或轨迹走到原方程没有定义的位置。课程中的椭圆上下半支在竖直切线处无法继续作为 $y(x)$ 解斜率方程；几何曲线仍可继续，却不能因此声称同一个一元函数解也能继续。方程本身的缺口见 [[方程未定义处分支独立]]。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Genuine obstructions include blow-up, failure of the required differentiability, and reaching a point outside the equation's domain. In the course's ellipse example, a branch cannot continue through a vertical tangent as a function $y(x)$ solving the slope equation, even though the geometric curve continues. A change of representation must not silently change the original problem.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 公式在某点分母为零，是否就已经找到了最大解区间的端点？
>
> **答案：** 还没有。先检查这是否只是表达式的可去缺口，解能否用其他表达继续满足原方程；只有实际的爆破、不可导或方程定义域障碍等，才阻止相应延拓。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> **Check:** Does finding a zero denominator in the formula establish an endpoint of the maximal solution interval?
>
> **Answer:** Not yet. Check whether the expression merely has a removable gap and whether another representation extends the solution of the original equation. A genuine obstruction, such as blow-up, failure of differentiability, or leaving the equation's domain, is needed to prevent that extension.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
