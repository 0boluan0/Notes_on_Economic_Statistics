# 量词次序不同通常改变命题含义
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Changing the order of different quantifiers usually changes a proposition's meaning*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 原子区别
> $\forall x\exists y\,P(x,y)$ 允许 $y$ 随 $x$ 改变；$\exists y\forall x\,P(x,y)$ 要求一个固定的 $y$ 同时适用于所有 $x$。把全称量词和存在量词交换，通常会改变命题。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> In $\forall x\exists y\,P(x,y)$, the choice of $y$ may depend on $x$. In $\exists y\forall x\,P(x,y)$, one fixed $y$ must work for every $x$. Exchanging universal and existential quantifiers therefore usually changes the claim.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

在整数论域中，$\forall x\exists y:y>x$ 为真：给定 $x$ 后可取 $y=x+1$。而 $\exists y\forall x:y>x$ 为假，因为不存在大于所有整数的固定整数。两式使用相同谓词，差异完全来自选择顺序与依赖关系。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Over the integers, $\forall x\exists y:y>x$ is true: after receiving $x$, choose $y=x+1$. But $\exists y\forall x:y>x$ is false because no fixed integer exceeds every integer. The predicate is identical; the difference lies entirely in the order of choices and permitted dependence.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

## 边界
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Boundary*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

- 相邻的同类量词可以交换，例如 $\forall x\forall y\,P(x,y)$ 与 $\forall y\forall x\,P(x,y)$ 等价；风险主要来自交换不同种类的量词。
- 写自然语言时应说明 witness 是否允许依赖先前给定的对象，否则“每个人都有一个选择”可能被误读成“所有人共享同一个选择”。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->

**1.** Adjacent quantifiers of the same kind may be exchanged: $\forall x\forall y\,P(x,y)$ is equivalent to $\forall y\forall x\,P(x,y)$. The main danger is swapping different kinds of quantifier.<br>
**2.** In prose, say whether a witness may depend on a previously given object; otherwise “everyone has a choice” may be misread as “there is one shared choice for everyone.”<br>
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 回忆与应用
> “每名学生都有一位导师”更自然对应哪一种结构？它是否推出“有一位导师指导所有学生”？
>
> **答案：** 它通常对应 $\forall s\exists t\,Mentors(t,s)$，导师可随学生改变；这不推出 $\exists t\forall s\,Mentors(t,s)$。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> Which quantifier structure naturally expresses “every student has an adviser”? Does it imply that one adviser supervises every student?
>
> **Answer:** It normally means $\forall s\exists t\,Mentors(t,s)$, where the adviser may vary with the student. It does not imply $\exists t\forall s\,Mentors(t,s)$.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
