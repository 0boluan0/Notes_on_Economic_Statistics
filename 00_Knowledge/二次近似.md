# 二次近似是在基点匹配函数值及前两阶导数的唯一次数不超过二的多项式
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*The quadratic approximation is the unique polynomial of degree at most two matching a function's value and first two derivatives at the base point*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 它是什么
> 若 $f''(a)$ 存在，则 $f$ 在 $a$ 的二次近似是
> $$
> Q_a(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)+\frac{f''(a)}{2}(x-a)^2.
> $$
> 它满足 $Q_a^{(k)}(a)=f^{(k)}(a)$，$k=0,1,2$。系数 $1/2$ 来自二次项求两次导产生的 $2!$，不是人为约定。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> If $f''(a)$ exists, the quadratic approximation is $Q_a(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)+\frac12f''(a)(x-a)^2$. It matches the function and its first two derivatives at $a$. The factor $1/2$ compensates for the $2!$ produced by differentiating a quadratic term twice.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如在零点，
$$
\ln(1+x)\approx x-\frac{x^2}{2}.
$$
取 $x=0.1$ 得 $0.095$，比线性估计 $0.1$ 更接近 $\ln1.1\approx0.09531$。改进来自二阶曲率信息，而不是“多写一项就一定更准”：离基点太远、函数不够光滑或高阶导数很大时，二次式仍可能很差。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
At zero, $\ln(1+x)\approx x-x^2/2$, giving $0.095$ at $x=0.1$, closer than the linear estimate $0.1$. The improvement comes from matching curvature, not from a universal rule that one extra term must be better. A distant target, insufficient smoothness, or large higher derivatives can still make the approximation poor.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

“二次近似”描述最高允许次数，不保证二次系数非零。$\sin x$ 在零点的二次近似仍是 $x$，因为 $f''(0)=0$。同时，$f''(a)>0$ 只说明拟合抛物线在该点开口向上；它不能单独证明函数在整个区间上凸。实际误差由 [[Taylor余项]] 的区间条件控制。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
“Quadratic” names the maximum permitted degree; its quadratic coefficient may vanish. The second-order approximation to $\sin x$ at zero is still $x$. Likewise, $f''(a)>0$ describes the fitted parabola at one point and does not prove convexity over an interval. The actual error requires an interval-based remainder bound.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 为什么不能把两个线性近似相乘后留下碰巧出现的某个 $x^2$ 项，并称之为二次近似？
>
> **答案：** 真正的二次近似必须匹配完整二阶导数；乘积中还可能有来自各因子二阶项的一阶乘常数贡献，单留一个交叉项会漏掉它们。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> **Check:** Why is retaining the $x^2$ term that happens to appear when multiplying two linear approximations insufficient to construct a quadratic approximation?
>
> **Answer:** A quadratic approximation must match the complete second derivative. Each factor's own quadratic term may also contribute when multiplied by the other factor's constant term; keeping only the cross term misses these contributions.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
