# 自归一化重要性抽样用非负重要性权重的加权和之比估计目标期望，并消去未知的共同归一化常数
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Self-normalised importance sampling estimates a target expectation by a ratio of nonnegative importance-weighted sums, cancelling an unknown common normalising constant*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 它是什么
> 当只能计算与 $p/q$ 成比例的非负权重 $\widetilde w$ 时，自归一化重要性抽样用权重总和归一化加权输出，使未知的共同常数在分子与分母中抵消。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> When nonnegative weights are known only up to a common constant, self-normalised importance sampling divides the weighted output sum by the weight sum so that the constant cancels.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对独立样本 $X_i\sim q$，定义

$$
\widehat\mu_{SNIS}
=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^N h(X_i)\widetilde w(X_i)}
{\sum_{i=1}^N \widetilde w(X_i)},
$$

前提是分母为正。若 $\widetilde w=c\,p/q$ 且 $c>0$，共同常数 $c$ 自动消去。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The estimator is defined when the weight sum is positive. Any common positive scale factor in the unnormalised weights cancels between numerator and denominator.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

若目标密度只能以未归一化形式 $\pi(x)=Zp(x)$ 计算，可以取

$$
\widetilde w(x)=\frac{\pi(x)}{q(x)}
=Z\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}.
$$

$Z$ 同时乘在分子和分母上，所以无需先知道 $Z$。这正是 SNIS 与普通重要性抽样加权平均的区别：前者是随机比率，后者在归一化目标密度可计算时直接平均 $h(X_i)p(X_i)/q(X_i)$。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
If only an unnormalised target $\pi=Zp$ is available, then $\widetilde w=\pi/q=Zp/q$. The same unknown constant multiplies both sums and therefore cancels. This makes SNIS a ratio estimator rather than the ordinary importance-sampling mean.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

随机比率的有限样本偏差由 [[自归一抽样有限样本偏差]] 单独承载；何时仍收敛到目标期望由 [[自归一抽样强一致性]] 承载。支持覆盖和二阶矩仍需分别检查，不能把“常数消掉”当作正确性与精度的全部证明。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Finite-sample bias and strong consistency are separate properties of this ratio estimator. Support coverage and weighted moments must still be checked rather than inferred from scale cancellation.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 可核验自检
> 若把每个未归一化权重都乘以 7，SNIS 估计值如何变化？
>
> **答案：** 不变；分子与分母同时乘以 7，公共因子相消。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> It does not change because the common factor of seven cancels from numerator and denominator.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
