# 通解描述微分方程的一族解而初值选择其中满足指定状态的解
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*A general solution describes a family of ODE solutions while an initial condition selects members with a specified state*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 原子区分
> 通解是某一区域内由参数表示的一族解；特解是其中一条具体轨迹；初值条件如 $y(x_0)=y_0$ 要求轨迹经过指定状态。把初值代入通解可以求参数，但“求出了参数”只证明候选满足初值，不自动证明存在唯一性或最大定义区间。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> A general solution is a parameterized family of solutions in a region; a particular solution is one trajectory; an initial condition such as $y(x_0)=y_0$ asks for a trajectory through a specified state. Substituting the condition may determine parameters, but it does not by itself prove existence, uniqueness, or a maximal interval.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

方程 $y'+xy=0$ 的解族为
$$
y=Ae^{-x^2/2}.
$$
初值 $y(0)=3$ 给 $A=3$；初值 $y(0)=0$ 给零解。任意常数不是“算不完”，而是方程只规定变化率，还缺少一个起始高度。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For $y'+xy=0$, the family is $y=Ae^{-x^2/2}$. The initial values $3$ and $0$ at zero select $A=3$ and the equilibrium solution respectively. The free constant records missing starting information rather than an unfinished calculation.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

一个 $n$ 阶方程通常需要 $n$ 个独立初始数据，但这是计数直觉，不是无条件定理。右端不连续、不满足适当的局部 Lipschitz 条件或初值落在奇点时，可能无解或多解。最终答案还要说明 [[微分方程最大区间]]，不能让公式越过原方程无定义的位置。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
An $n$th-order equation typically needs $n$ independent initial data, but this is a counting guide rather than an unconditional theorem. Irregular right-hand sides or singular initial points may produce no solution or multiple solutions. A complete answer also states the maximal interval and does not extend a formula across points where the equation is undefined.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 初值代入后唯一算出常数，为什么仍不能直接宣称初值问题唯一？
>
> **答案：** 所写参数族可能并未包含所有解，或方程在该点不满足唯一性条件；代数只在候选族内部完成选择。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> **Check:** Why does uniquely determining the constant from initial data not by itself prove that the initial value problem has a unique solution?
>
> **Answer:** The written family may not contain every solution, or the equation may fail the uniqueness hypotheses at that point. The algebra has selected a member only within the candidate family.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
