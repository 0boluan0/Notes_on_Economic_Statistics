# 剩余劳动是超过再生产劳动力价值所需劳动的工作日部分
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Surplus labour is the part of the working day beyond the labour needed to reproduce labour-power's value*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

剩余劳动（surplus labour）是在工作日中超过 [[必要劳动]] 的劳动。在资本主义生产关系下，这部分劳动创造由资本占有的 [[剩余价值]]；其时长称为剩余劳动时间。若工作日时长为 $T$、必要劳动时间为 $t_n$、剩余劳动时间为 $t_s$，则有：
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Surplus labour is the labour performed during the working day beyond [[必要劳动|necessary labour]]. Under capitalist production relations it creates [[剩余价值|surplus value]] appropriated by capital. Its duration is surplus labour time. If $T$ is the working day's duration, $t_n$ necessary labour time and $t_s$ surplus labour time, then:
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$$
T=t_n+t_s,\qquad t_s=T-t_n.
$$

在教学构造中，劳动力按其价值出售，社会劳动折算与劳动强度保持一致；8小时工作日中，4小时创造劳动力价值的等价物，剩下4小时就是剩余劳动。工人整天可以做同一种纺纱劳动；必要与剩余的差别由新增价值的归属来界定。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
In the constructed example, labour-power sells at its value and social-labour conversion and intensity remain consistent. Four hours of an eight-hour day create an equivalent of labour-power's value, leaving four hours of surplus labour. The worker may spin throughout the day. The distinction concerns the appropriation of the new value created.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

剩余劳动有比资本主义更广的历史范围：Marx 也用它分析奴隶或农奴为占有生产资料的人提供的劳动。不同社会关系区别在于这种劳动怎样被提取。在当前资本主义分析中，必须进一步解释它为什么表现为剩余价值；这一环节见 [[等价交换与剩余]]。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Surplus labour has a wider historical scope than capitalism: Marx also applies it to labour performed by slaves or serfs for those who possess the means of production. Social relations differ in how that labour is extracted. In the current analysis of capitalism, its appearance as surplus value needs further explanation; see [[等价交换与剩余|surplus production under exchange of equivalents]].
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

Volume I, Chapter 9, Sections 1 and 4 support the definition, the relation to surplus value and the division of the working day. The comparison of spinning and jewellery-making at the end of Chapter 7, Section 2 supports the qualitative identity of labour in the two portions. The opening of Chapter 10, Section 2 establishes the wider historical scope. The MIA text uses the Moore and Aveling translation edited by Engels, based on the 1887 first English edition with indicated fourth German edition changes. The eight-hour illustration is constructed.
