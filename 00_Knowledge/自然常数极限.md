# A limit for the number e
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

*自然常数极限*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Multiply by $1+1/n$, and repeat that multiplication $n$ times. Each individual increase becomes smaller as the positive integer $n$ grows, but there are more increases. Their combined factor approaches $e\approx2.71828$, the base of the [[自然指数函数|natural exponential function]]. It does not approach one merely because each individual factor does.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

把 $1+1/n$ 连乘 $n$ 次。正整数 $n$ 越大，每一次增加的比例越小，但增加的次数也越多。总倍数趋近[[自然指数函数]]的底数 $e\approx2.71828$，并不会因为每个因子趋近一，就也趋近一。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For example, the first two choices give $2^1=2$ and $(3/2)^2=2.25$. With ten repetitions the factor is about $2.59374$; with one hundred it is about $2.70481$. These calculations illustrate the pattern. The limit needs a proof that covers arbitrarily large $n$.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如，前两种选择给出 $2^1=2$ 与 $(3/2)^2=2.25$。重复十次时总倍数约为 $2.59374$，一百次时约为 $2.70481$。这些计算展示了趋势；极限本身还需要覆盖任意大 $n$ 的证明。

> [!abstract] Small changes, many repetitions · 小变化累积很多次
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> For positive integers $n$, the [[序列收敛|sequence limit]] is
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 对正整数 $n$，相应的[[序列收敛|数列极限]]为
>
> $$\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n=e.$$
>
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> A related [[函数极限|function limit]] allows a real increment $h$ from either side of zero:
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 对应的[[函数极限]]允许实数增量 $h$ 从零的两侧趋近：
>
> $$\lim_{h\to0}(1+h)^{1/h}=e,\qquad h\ne0,\quad 1+h>0.$$
>
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> The positive base makes the real power meaningful. At $h=0$ the displayed expression is undefined; the limit describes nearby values. Substituting $h=1/n$ uses only the positive side and gives the sequence above.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> 底数为正，实数次幂才按这里的方式定义。$h=0$ 时这个表达式没有定义，极限描述的是附近的值。代入 $h=1/n$ 只使用正侧，就得到上面的数列。

## Turn the exponent into a difference quotient
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

*把变化的指数转成差商*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The [[自然对数|natural logarithm]] satisfies $\ln(u^r)=r\ln u$ for $u>0$ and real $r$. Thus it brings the moving exponent down as a multiplier. Put $y(h)=(1+h)^{1/h}>0$. Since $\ln1=0$,
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对 $u>0$ 和实数 $r$，[[自然对数]]满足 $\ln(u^r)=r\ln u$，能把变化的指数移到前面变成乘数。令 $y(h)=(1+h)^{1/h}>0$。利用 $\ln1=0$，

$$\ln y(h)=\frac{\ln(1+h)}h
=\frac{\ln(1+h)-\ln1}{h}.$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The last expression is exactly the [[导数|difference quotient]] of $\ln$ at input one: a change in its output divided by the input change $h$. The [[对数函数求导|logarithmic derivative]] is $1/x$ for $x>0$, so this quotient tends to $1/1=1$. To recover $y$, use $y(h)=e^{\ln y(h)}$ and the continuity of the exponential:
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

最后一个表达式正是 $\ln$ 在输入一处的[[导数|差商]]：输出的变化除以输入的变化 $h$。[[对数函数求导|对数导数]]在 $x>0$ 时为 $1/x$，因此这个差商趋于 $1/1=1$。最后用 $y(h)=e^{\ln y(h)}$ 和指数函数的连续性还原：

$$\ln y(h)\longrightarrow1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y(h)=e^{\ln y(h)}\longrightarrow e^1=e.$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
This last step proves the limit of $y$ exists. It does not assume that existence by writing $\ln(\lim y)$ first. Also, $\ln(1+h)/h$ has the same limit for small negative $h$; a negative exponent causes no problem when $1+h$ remains positive.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

最后这一步同时证明了 $y$ 的极限存在，并不是先写 $\ln(\lim y)$ 来假定它已经存在。小的负 $h$ 也给出同一个差商极限；只要 $1+h$ 仍为正，负指数并不会造成问题。

> [!warning] The base alone does not determine the answer · 不能只看底数
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> The label $1^\infty$ describes competing limits, not an arithmetic value. For example, $(1+1/n)^{2n}\to e^2$, although its base has the same limit one. The changing exponent must remain in the calculation.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
>
> $1^\infty$ 是描述两个量同时变化的未定式标签，不是一个算术值。例如 $(1+1/n)^{2n}\to e^2$，尽管它的底数仍趋于一。计算中必须保留变化的指数。

## Fixed changes of scale
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

*固定尺度的改变*

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For fixed real constants $c,d$, the base $1+c/n$ is positive for all sufficiently large $n$, and
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

对固定实常数 $c,d$，当 $n$ 足够大时 $1+c/n$ 为正，且

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac cn\right)^{dn}=e^{cd}.$$

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
If $c\ne0$, set $h=c/n$ and calculate $dn\ln(1+c/n)=cd\,\ln(1+h)/h\to cd$. If $c=0$, the expression is identically one, agreeing with $e^0$. In particular, $(1-1/n)^n\to e^{-1}$ with $n\ge2$. The same calculation underlies [[连续复利指数关系|continuous compounding]]; it is a limit of finite repetition, not an equality for a fixed finite number of repetitions.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

若 $c\ne0$，令 $h=c/n$，得到 $dn\ln(1+c/n)=cd\,\ln(1+h)/h\to cd$。若 $c=0$，原式恒为一，与 $e^0$ 一致。特别地，$(1-1/n)^n\to e^{-1}$，这里从 $n\ge2$ 起取正底数。同一个计算也解释[[连续复利指数关系]]：它是有限次重复的极限，并不是对固定有限次数的精确等式。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The proof uses an independently established exponential and logarithm. In the [[自然指数函数|construction of the natural exponential]], one can first form $L(u)=\int_1^u dt/t$, invert this increasing function, and set $e=\exp(1)$. The logarithmic derivative then follows without using the limit proved here. This is why the present argument is not circular. One may instead start a theory by defining $e$ through the sequence, but then its existence and the derivative formulas must be established independently in that order.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

这个证明使用已经独立建立的指数与对数。在[[自然指数函数]]的构造中，可以先定义 $L(u)=\int_1^u dt/t$，再取这个递增函数的反函数，并令 $e=\exp(1)$；对数导数的建立不需要这里待证的数列极限，因此论证不循环。也可以选择先用数列定义 $e$ 的另一套理论顺序，但那时必须先独立证明极限存在，再建立后续求导公式。
