# 连续函数在闭区间上的绝对极值可由端点与内部临界点的函数值比较确定
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Absolute extrema of a continuous function on a closed interval are found by comparing endpoint and interior critical values*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!summary] 原子方法
> 对连续函数 $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$：列出 $a,b$；找出 $(a,b)$ 内所有满足 $f'=0$ 或 $f'$ 不存在而 $f$ 有定义的临界点；计算每个候选的函数值；最大与最小输出分别给出绝对极值。[[极值定理]] 保证至少有答案，[[无约束一阶条件]] 说明光滑内点为何必须进入候选表。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> For a continuous function on $[a,b]$, list both endpoints and every interior point where the derivative is zero or fails to exist while the function is defined. Evaluate the function at all candidates and compare the outputs. The extreme value theorem guarantees attainment, while the first-order necessary condition explains why every smooth interior extremum appears in the list.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->

例如 $f(x)=x^3-3x$ 在 $[-2,3]$ 上有 $f'(x)=3(x^2-1)$，候选为 $-2,-1,1,3$。其函数值分别为 $-2,2,-2,18$，所以绝对最小值为 $-2$，在 $x=-2$ 与 $x=1$ 两处取得；绝对最大值为 $18$，在 $x=3$ 取得。只解 $f'=0$ 会同时漏掉最大点和一个最小点。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For $f(x)=x^3-3x$ on $[-2,3]$, the candidates are $-2,-1,1,3$, with values $-2,2,-2,18$. The absolute minimum is attained at two points, and the absolute maximum occurs at the right endpoint. Solving only $f'=0$ misses both an absolute maximum and one point attaining the minimum.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

方法有明确边界。若区间不闭、函数不连续或候选无限多，极值定理或有限比较步骤可能失效；这不等于极值必定不存在，而是需要极限、单调性、紧致性或其他论证。定义域外的间断边界只能用单侧极限分析，不能当作函数点代入。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The method has boundaries. If the interval is not closed, the function is discontinuous, or the candidate set is infinite, the attainment theorem or finite comparison may no longer apply. Extrema may still exist, but require another argument. A boundary outside the domain is analyzed by one-sided limits, not evaluated as a function point.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

> [!question]- 自检
> 为什么闭区间法既需要“存在性定理”又需要“候选点定理”？
>
> **答案：** 极值定理保证最大、最小会取得；一阶必要条件和不可导检查保证取得位置不会从候选表中漏掉。两者回答不同问题。
> <!-- bilingual-en:start -->
> **Check:** Why does the closed-interval method need both an attainment theorem and a theorem locating candidates?
>
> **Answer:** The extreme value theorem guarantees attained maxima and minima. The first-order necessary condition, together with checks for nondifferentiable points, prevents their locations from being omitted. These results answer different questions.
> <!-- bilingual-en:end -->
