# 潜在补偿准则要求新状态下存在 Pareto 优于原状态的可行再分配
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*The potential compensation criterion requires a redistribution feasible in the new state that Pareto-improves on the old state.*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

潜在补偿准则考察：改变之后，是否存在一种允许的再分配，使每个人至少与改变前一样好，且至少一人更好。它只要求这种补偿可行，不要求补偿已发生。本卡采用常见“Kaldor–Hicks 潜在改进”简写中的正向可补偿口径，即 Kaldor 检验；历史上的 Hicks 反向检验另见 [[卡尔多与希克斯检验]]。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The criterion asks whether an admissible redistribution after the change could leave everyone no worse off than before, with someone strictly better off. Feasibility is not actual payment. Here the common “Kaldor–Hicks potential improvement” shorthand denotes the forward Kaldor test; [[卡尔多与希克斯检验|the separate Hicks test]] checks the reverse direction.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

记原状态为 $A$、新状态为 $B$，$C(B)$ 是在新状态下允许转移所能达到的配置集合，$\succ_P$ 表示 [[Pareto改进与有效|Pareto 改进]]。检验为：
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Let $A$ be the old state, $B$ the new state, and $C(B)$ the allocations attainable through permitted transfers after the change. Let $\succ_P$ mean a [[Pareto改进与有效|Pareto improvement]]:
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$$
\exists\,\widetilde B\in C(B)
\quad\text{such that}\quad
\widetilde B\succ_P A.
$$

例如在效用可用稳定货币金额表达、转移无成本且无约束的自造例中，甲得益 $100$、乙损失 $60$。不转移时不是 Pareto 改进；甲转给乙 $60$ 后，甲净得 $40$、乙不变，因此存在潜在补偿。转移 $80$ 则两人各得 $20$。这些结论依赖给定的可转移货币收益，不是把任意个人效用数值直接相加。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
In a constructed example with stable money-metric gains and costless, unrestricted transfers, one person gains $100$ and another loses $60$. A payment of $60$ leaves gains of $40$ and zero; a payment of $80$ leaves each $20$ better off. Without payment, the actual change is not a Pareto improvement. This uses transferable monetary gains, not a sum of arbitrary utility numbers.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

应用时必须说明允许的转移、价格和收入基准，以及补偿是否真的执行。通常的理想检验设想无成本一次总付再分配；若现实实施有成本或限制，可行集合会改变。通过检验本身既不消除实际受损者，也不提供完整的分配判断或所有政策的总排序。
<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Specify permitted transfers, the price and income baseline, and whether compensation is implemented. The ideal test commonly assumes costless lump-sum redistribution; implementation costs or constraints change feasibility. Passing it neither removes actual losers nor supplies a complete distributional judgement or policy ranking.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
