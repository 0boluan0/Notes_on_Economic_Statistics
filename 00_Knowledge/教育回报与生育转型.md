# 技术变迁若提高教育回报并使育儿投入转向质量，可以推动生育下降

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
*Technological change can lower fertility if it raises educational returns and shifts child-related investment towards quality.*
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

在 Galor–Weil（2000）的统一增长模型中，持续技术变迁提高适应新技术的教育回报，家庭因而可能把育儿投入从子女数量转向每名子女的教育。这是[[生育数量质量权衡]]产生生育转型的一条有条件机制，不是“人均收入提高自然使所有家庭少生”的定律。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
In Galor and Weil's (2000) unified growth model, ongoing technological change raises the educational return to adapting to new technology. Families can consequently shift child-related investment from quantity towards education per child. This is a conditional mechanism based on the [[生育数量质量权衡|quantity–quality trade-off]], not a universal consequence of higher income per capita.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

模型保留两个不同方向的假设。人口规模促进技术进步，成人教育也促进下一期技术进步；反过来，预期技术进步越快，教育帮助子女适应新环境的边际价值越高。作者把人力资本写成 $h(e,g)$：教育投入 $e$ 提高人力资本，但有递减边际回报；技术进步率 $g$ 会侵蚀既有知识的适用性，而教育缓解这种侵蚀。这不否定技术对生产的直接改善。作者另假设，技术不变时开始教育的边际收益不足以补偿其时间成本；结合函数形状条件，存在门槛 $\hat g$：不超过门槛时最优教育投入为零，超过门槛后才随 $g$ 上升。教育与技术由此能够相互强化；这些技术与教育关系是模型假设，不是由预算恒等式推出。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
The model assumes that population size and adult education promote technological progress, while faster expected technological change makes education more valuable for adapting to new conditions. Human capital $h(e,g)$ rises with educational investment at a diminishing rate. Technological change erodes existing knowledge, and education mitigates that erosion; this does not negate technology's direct benefit to production. The model also assumes that starting education is not worth its time cost when technology is unchanged. Together with the shape conditions, this gives a threshold $\hat g$: education is zero at or below it and increases with $g$ above it. Education and technology can then reinforce each other; neither direction follows from a budget identity alone.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

生育下降还要经过家庭时间预算。每位模型家长有一单位成人时间，每名子女耗时 $\tau^q+\tau^e e$，其中 $\tau^q,\tau^e>0$。设潜在收入 $z$ 为全部时间用于劳动时的收入，最低消费要求为 $\bar c>0$，$0<\gamma<1$ 为效用中的子女权重。对 $z>\bar c$，最优子女数量可写为

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Fertility responds through the household time budget. Each model parent has one unit of adult time, and each child requires $\tau^q+\tau^e e$, with positive cost parameters. Potential income $z$ is income from working the entire time endowment, $\bar c>0$ is minimum required consumption, and $0<\gamma<1$ is the utility weight on children. For $z>\bar c$, optimal fertility can be written as:
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$$
n=\frac{b(z)}{\tau^q+\tau^e e},
\qquad
b(z)=\min\left\{\gamma,\ 1-\frac{\bar c}{z}\right\}.
$$

当 $z\ge\bar c/(1-\gamma)$ 时，生存消费约束不再挤压育儿投入，总育儿时间固定为 $b(z)=\gamma$。此时最优教育 $e$ 上升会提高每名子女耗时，因而降低 $n$。构造例子：取 $\gamma=0.3$、$\tau^q=0.1$、$\tau^e=0.05$；若教育选择由 $e=0$ 升至 $e=1$，则 $n$ 从 $3$ 降至 $2$，总育儿时间均为 $0.3$。这里的 $n$ 是连续选择模型中每位家长的子女数量，不是统计上的总和生育率；数例只展示时间分配，并未估计何种技术变化会触发这次教育调整。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
When $z\ge\bar c/(1-\gamma)$, subsistence no longer compresses child-related investment and total parenting time is fixed at $\gamma$. Higher chosen education then raises time per child and reduces $n$. For example, with $\gamma=0.3$, $\tau^q=0.1$, and $\tau^e=0.05$, a change from $e=0$ to $e=1$ lowers $n$ from $3$ to $2$, keeping total parenting time at $0.3$. This continuous-choice quantity per model parent is not the statistical total fertility rate, and the example does not estimate a technology shock.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

在生存约束仍起作用时，收入增加会提高 $b(z)$，从而支持更多子女；技术进步的这项资源效应可与转向教育的效应相抵。固定预期技术进步时，模型中的潜在收入增加不改变教育选择；在上述收入阈值之上，继续增加潜在收入也不改变子女数量。因此，要分开“技术改变教育回报”与[[生育的工资与收入效应|收入或工资变化]]。作者也明确：其欧洲历史转型叙事不能完整套到当代发展中国家，后者能够进口既有技术和健康技术，不一定依靠同样的人口规模启动机制。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
While subsistence binds, higher income raises $b(z)$ and supports more children, potentially offsetting the shift towards education. Holding expected technological progress fixed, higher potential income does not change education; above the income threshold it does not change fertility either. Changing educational returns must therefore be distinguished from [[生育的工资与收入效应|income or wage changes]]. The authors also caution that their European historical account does not fully apply to contemporary developing countries, which can import existing production and health technologies without the same population-driven take-off mechanism.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
