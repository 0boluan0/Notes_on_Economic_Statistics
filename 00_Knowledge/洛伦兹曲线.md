# 洛伦兹曲线表示按收入从低到高排列的人口累计份额所获得的累计收入份额

洛伦兹曲线（Lorenz curve）先把人口按收入从低到高排列，再以累计人口份额为横轴、该部分人口获得的累计收入份额为纵轴，描述收入的相对分布。消费分布也可按同样方式构造，但比较时须统一[[贫困测量的福利口径|收入或消费的福利口径]]。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
A Lorenz curve orders people from lowest to highest income and plots their cumulative population share against their cumulative share of total income. It describes relative distribution. The same construction applies to consumption, but comparisons must use a consistent [[贫困测量的福利口径|income or consumption welfare measure]].
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

设 $n$ 人等权、收入非负且总收入为正，排序后为 $y_{(1)}\le\cdots\le y_{(n)}$。曲线经过以下点，有限人口例中相邻点用直线连接：

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For $n$ equally weighted people with nonnegative incomes and positive total income, order incomes as $y_{(1)}\le\cdots\le y_{(n)}$. Plot the following points and join adjacent points by straight lines in the finite-population example:
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

$$
L(0)=0,\qquad
L\!\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{(i)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{(i)}},
\quad k=1,\ldots,n.
$$

例如四人收入为 $10,20,30,40$，曲线经过 $(0,0)$、$(0.25,0.10)$、$(0.50,0.30)$、$(0.75,0.60)$、$(1,1)$。其中 $L(0.5)=0.3$ 表示收入较低的一半人口得到总收入的 30%，不是其收入等于某个固定金额。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
For incomes $10,20,30,40$, the points are $(0,0)$, $(0.25,0.10)$, $(0.50,0.30)$, $(0.75,0.60)$ and $(1,1)$. Here $L(0.5)=0.3$ means that the lower-income half receives 30% of total income, not a fixed monetary amount.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->

完全平等时 $L(p)=p$；上述条件下曲线不会高于这条平等线。曲线若交叉，便不能只凭“哪条整体更靠近平等线”给出一致排序；[[基尼系数]]把曲线与平等线之间的面积压成一个数。所有收入同比例增加时曲线不变，因此它不单独显示收入水平。调查数据要使用人口权重；若存在负收入或总收入为零，上述常规解释不能直接照搬。

<!-- bilingual-en:start -->
Perfect equality gives $L(p)=p$; under these assumptions the curve cannot lie above that equality line. Crossing curves do not admit an unambiguous ordering by which curve lies everywhere closer to equality. The [[基尼系数|Gini coefficient]] summarizes the area between the curve and the equality line. Proportional growth of every income leaves the curve unchanged, so it does not separately reveal income levels. Survey data require population weights. Negative incomes or zero total income require reconsidering these standard interpretations.
<!-- bilingual-en:end -->
